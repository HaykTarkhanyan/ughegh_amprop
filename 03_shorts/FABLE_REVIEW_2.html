0. How to read this, and the length table
  • Every item you may act on has an ID, F153 to F238, continuing the first review. Answer by ID.
  • Each item is tagged must (fix or decide before the voice is made), should (makes the video clearly better) or could (optional).
  • Armenian items are questions, never rewrites. [heard] = structural, about following it by ear (sentence length, ambiguity): fairly reliable. [ear] = "sounds off to me": right about 1 time in 9, so there are very few. "Checkable" = a repeated word, an unclear referent, a mismatch between two beats.
  • "Gloss" = an English version of a possible line. The Armenian wording is yours.
  • Lengths are the length lines in each PLAN.md (tools/plan_script.py), not recounted. Beat start times were computed with the same rate (870 characters a minute, pauses included) only to say at what second a payoff lands.
  • Verdicts are about the script. ready = can be voiced as written, the findings are improvements. small fixes = do the must and should items first. rework = the structure needs a decision.
  • Each section ends with my pick on that plan's open U questions.

If you read only one screen

  • The batch is clearly better than the first one. The four top changes of the first review mostly landed: no «Այնպես որ,» ending (0 of 11, was 11 of 11), no summary cards, 6 spoken years (was 33), every hook opens on the claim, 9 of 11 have a local anchor, pauses are marked. The first payoff comes at 0:06-0:46 (the first batch: 0:32-1:15).
  • Ready: decoy_popcorn, map_projection, zero_knowledge, painted_statues. Small fixes: week_seven, mashtots, english_spelling, dmanisi, ikea_effect, minute_60 (in that order of strength). Rework: luggage_wheels.
  • The eight musts: F157 (dmanisi: tag the painting), F164 (mashtots: one TTS test), F170 and F172 (english_spelling: the takeaway contradicts beat 6; the claim about Armenian spelling), F178 (week_seven: a missing link in the showcase), F212 (painted_statues: tag the reconstructions), F216 (luggage_wheels: the topic), F231 (the plans' own open "check before voicing" flags).
  • The new habits this batch grew (section 12): an eight-beat mold, a "doubt" slot before the ending, the same "they tried to change it and failed" beat in all three origin shorts, a "not A, but B" closing line in 6 of 11, and pauses that are marked but tiny (2 s a script on average).
  • Top changes: F234-F238.

What the numbers say

  1. Nothing is near the cap. The longest is 2:14; the ceiling is 2:45 of speech. No forced cuts. The cuts I recommend below are all editorial.
  2. The batch is half a minute shorter than the first one: about 1:55 on average against about 2:27 (the two reviews used slightly different estimates, so treat it as "clearly shorter"). Seven of eleven are under 2:00, the low end of what you asked for. My advice: do not fill the gap with more facts. Fill it with silence and with moments where the viewer does something (F222).
  3. Pauses: 21.5 s in the whole batch, about 2 s a script, in 10 of 11 scripts (dmanisi has none). The first review asked for 10-15 s in a showcase. There is now 45-80 s of headroom in every script, so this is free.
  4. Sentences are easier by ear than in the first batch: the average is 55-68 characters in every script (kids_tv was 81). The long ones are listed per script; the longest is 163 characters (map_projection beat 7).
  5. Numbers: five scripts write digits, six write numbers as words (F229).
1. dmanisi

10 beats, 1,903 characters, about 132 s of speech, about 2:14. No marked pauses.

Verdict: small fixes (two cuts). One idea, the channel's own, and the best closing line of all 22 scripts. The problem is proportion: the story gets 16 s and the doubt gets 75 s.

What works. A real skull in frame 0. The "how do we know" beat (sockets fill with bone, and that takes years) comes before the story, so the viewer trusts the fact before he hears the interpretation. The neighbour anchor in beat 2 is natural, not bolted on. The three short sentences in beat 5 («Բայց ի՞նչ է ասում ինքը՝ ոսկորը։ Այն ասում է, որ նա ապրել է առանց ատամների։ Այն չի ասում՝ ինչպես։») are the turn of the video and need nothing.

Hook: strong. By 3 s the viewer has "almost two million years ago a man lost" plus the skull; the teeth land at about 4 s and the question at 8 s. The first sentence is 5.2 s, but it is the claim, not a lead-in. One risk: «Ինչպե՞ս» promises an answer and the video ends on "we do not know which" (the yawning problem, F112). Here it mostly works, because the viewer guesses "someone fed him", has that confirmed at 0:32 and is turned around at 0:49. Keep it.

Structure and payoff. The story lands at 0:32-0:48, the turn comes at 0:49. Then 75 s that all say "not so sure": the bone does not say how; the authors raised both readings; a bonobo managed; the Mead quote is unverified; Neanderthal care is disputed; one bone cannot tell; we do not know which. Seven statements of doubt in a row. It is pattern 8 of the first review (honesty becoming the content), and it survives only because here the doubt is the point. It should be one clean turn and one piece of evidence, not five.

  • F153 (should) Cut beat 7 (mead, 19 s, the longest beat). It brings in a person and a quote and then takes the quote back. If the viewer has never seen the quote, he gains nothing (the qwerty-hook fault). If he has, it is a second debunk inside a video that has been debunking for 30 s. It is also a different lesson (a misattributed quote) from this video's (reading too much into evidence). The quote is material for its own short. Question: has your audience seen the femur quote (it circulates in Russian)? If yes and you want it, one sentence, not three.
  • F154 (should) Cut beat 8 (still), or fold one clause of it into beat 9. It asks "so is compassion made up?", answers "no", and then backs the "no" with evidence it calls disputed in the same sentence: claim and undo in one breath (kids_tv beats 7-9 did this). Beat 9 already guards against the wrong conclusion («Երկու պատմությունն էլ ուշագրավ են»). Also beats 8, 9 and 10 each end on a closing line ("one bone does not tell us everything" / "the honest thing is to say we do not know which" / the takeaway): three landings in a row. With F153 and F154 the order is hook, find, sockets, story, bone_says, bonobo, both, takeaway.
  • F155 (should) Mark pauses; there are none. The takeaway says we want beautiful stories to be true, so the viewer has to want it first: 2-2.5 s on the painting after beat 4, before «Բայց». Then 1 s after «Այն չի ասում՝ ինչպես», and 1.5 s after «չգիտենք որը». About 5 s in all.
  • F156 (could) Beat 6 opens with «2020 թվականին հետազոտողները նկարագրեցին»: a year and "researchers described" as the first words, the bibliography opener of the first batch (patterns 5 and 6). The source line has both. Open on the animal.

Ending. Lands, and it is earned. «Ոչ թե որովհետև դրանք սխալ են, այլ որովհետև շատ ենք ուզում, որ ճիշտ լինեն» is the sentence a viewer will repeat. It needs the pause before it (F155) and one landing before it, not three (F154).

Pauses and visuals. Mostly stills and cards; the only moving demonstration is the sockets filling with bone.

  • F157 (must; the channel's own honesty rule) The most persuasive picture in the video is a painting of the story that the video then questions. Tag it on screen as an artist's picture (gloss: "an artist's reconstruction"), the way the pilot's red-dot plane carries «նկարազարդում». The same for the two drawn scenes in beat 9.
  • F158 (could) Give the second half one picture that stays, instead of five cards (two labels, a bonobo, a quote card, a skeleton with a tipping scale, two scenes). Keep the skull on screen from beat 5 with two columns: "the bone says" (one solid line that never grows) and "we add" (the stories, dashed). Each new item lands in a column, and the viewer sees the takeaway before he hears it.

Length. Fine. About 1:45-1:50 after the two cuts and the pauses.

Armenian (questions).

  • F159 [heard] Beat 5, last sentence: 157 characters, about 11 s, two clauses joined by «բայց նաև» and an example at the end. Two sentences?
  • F160 (checkable) «Գրեթե» in beat 1 and «համարյա» in beat 3 (F126; the other ten scripts of this batch use only «գրեթե»). «ազգական» has two referents: «մեր հեռավոր ազգականն էր» (the viewer's kin, beat 2) and «ծերացած ազգականի մասին» (the helper's kin, beat 4). «կարեկցանք» means compassion here and carried "empathy" in yawning (F119): one answer decides both.

U questions, my pick. U-ii: keep the honest angle, shorter (F153, F154). U-kk: yes, credit the video in the description.

2. mashtots

10 beats, 1,806 characters, about 125 s of speech, about 2:11, 3.5 s of marked pauses.

Verdict: small fixes (order). The highest ceiling in the batch for this audience: an identity topic with three things most viewers were not taught at school (why Greek letters would not do, the Greek order that was kept, the letters as numbers). Two risks: the first 5 seconds ("I know this") and an ending that peaks one beat too early.

What works. «Փորձիր հունական տառերով գրել «ճնճղուկ» բառը» with a pause is the best single line in the batch: the viewer runs the experiment himself. The letter names said aloud (այբ, բեն, գիմ, դա next to ալֆա, բետա, գամմա, դելտա) make the borrowing audible with no screen at all. «Թագավորությունը շուտով կորավ, բայց գիրը մնաց» is the emotional peak. The hook's "even a first sentence" is paid at 1:19.

Hook: ok. By 3 s the viewer has the whole first sentence ("most alphabets have no author"). "Ours has one" lands at about 9 s and the question at 12.8 s: the longest hook in the batch, four sentences.

  • F161 (should) Two things. (a) The closing question ("why would a person sit down and invent letters?") has a school answer every Armenian viewer already knows; the fresh material is the how. Promise that. (b) «զրոյից» oversells: beat 5 shows he kept the Greek order and built on it. That is the most interesting beat and the hook denies it in advance. Two ways out: promise the how (gloss: "Ours has an author, a year and a first sentence. And he did not start from nothing: here is what he kept from the Greeks and what he had to invent."), or open on the ճնճղուկ challenge itself as frame 0 and second 0, since it is this script's own unique hook, and give the "author, year, first sentence" line right after. Either way, under 9 s.

Structure and payoff. School history at 0:13-0:28 (the part viewers know, and the slowest stretch). ճնճղուկ at 0:28-0:41. The showcase (the two rows) at 0:49-1:11. The first sentence at 1:19. The peak line at 1:45. Then Korea and the Cherokee at 1:46-2:02, then the takeaway.

  • F162 (should) Beat 9 (others) sits between the peak line and the takeaway and lets the air out. It also does not do what the plan says ("others did the same for the same reason"): the beat gives no reason, only two unrelated curiosities with two names nobody will keep. My pick: cut it (about 1:55 left) and end result -> takeaway; Hangul's mouth-shaped letters deserve their own short, not 8 seconds. Second choice: keep one of the two and move it up to right after beat 5, so the video still ends at home (U-nn).
  • F163 (should) Beat 5 (design) is the showcase and the densest beat: 21.5 s with a year, a region, a helper's name, a count, the principle and eight letter names, and no pause. Move «հույն գրիչ Հռոփանոսի օգնությամբ» and the cities to the source line, split the beat after «մեկ հնչյուն, մեկ տառ», and mark 2 s after the Greek-order sentence so the rows can link. The year 405 can stay: every viewer knows it and the hook promised it.
  • F164 (must before voicing; one cheap TTS call) Test what the voice may mangle: the classical sentence in beat 7 (U-mm), the letter names in beats 3 and 5 («ծա, ձա, ճե, ջե, ղատ, ռա»; «այբ, բեն, գիմ, դա»), and «Հռոփանոս», «Սեջոն», «Սեքվոյա» if they stay. If the classical line comes out wrong, voice only the modern version and show the classical one written.
  • F165 (should; on its face, not checked) Frame 0 shows Latin, Greek and Cyrillic with "?" as the author. Viewers who went to a post-Soviet school "know" that Cyrillic has named authors and will say so. Use alphabets nobody attributes to a person, or drop Cyrillic from the frame.
  • F166 (could) Two short side beats. Beat 4 (daniel, 8 s) is a second "not enough" right after ճնճղուկ; it can go, or stay as the one detail viewers remember from school. Beat 6 (numbers, 8 s) is a side fact as written. It becomes part of the design if the picture is the 36 letters as a 4 x 9 grid (units, tens, hundreds, thousands), which also shows why 36 is a tidy number. The grid is from general knowledge: check before use.

Ending. Lands. The takeaway repeats the hook's first two sentences almost word for word, which is fine as a loop, and «Մերը որոշում էր» is repeatable. It is weaker than the line before beat 9; after F162 the two sit next to each other and carry each other.

Pauses and visuals. Both pauses are on the right beats (the viewer tries ճնճղուկ; the first sentence is written out). The showcase has none (F163). The Greek and Armenian rows with the new letters dropping in are the picture of this video and will be worth the work.

Length. Fine at 2:11; about 1:55-2:00 after F162 with the extra pause.

Armenian (questions).

  • F167 (checkable) Beat 2 moves from the present («բաժանված է», «կարդում են», «չեն հասկանում») to the past («քարոզում էր», «տեսնում էր») inside one beat. Intended?

U questions, my pick. U-mm: both versions if the test passes (F164). U-nn: cut, or one example moved up (F162). U-oo: leave the Georgian claim out, agreed.

3. english_spelling

9 beats, 1,746 characters, about 120 s of speech, about 2:05, 2 s of marked pauses.

Verdict: small fixes. A clear, repeatable idea, the best image in words in the batch («հին արտասանության լուսանկար») and a takeaway people will quote («սխալ չէ՝ այն հին է»). Two musts: the takeaway contradicts beat 6, and the claim about Armenian will be challenged.

What works. «քնիխտ» said aloud. "Printing arrived exactly while the sounds were moving" is a mechanism, not a list of facts. Keeping English out of the voice works: the transliterations are fun to hear.

Hook: ok. By 3 s the viewer has "English children learn to read two..."; the comparison lands at 6.5 s (the longest first sentence in the batch), and the second sentence gives the cause. So by 0:10 the hook has asked and answered its own question. The open question (why is the spelling like this?) is never said.

  • F168 (should) Open on beat 2. The five -ough words are the better frame 0 and the better first 3 seconds: every viewer who learned English at school has suffered from them, while the reading speed of Scottish first-graders is far from him. Then the statistic as the consequence (gloss: "that is why English children need twice as long to learn to read"), then «Բայց մի ժամանակ ...».
  • F169 (should) Beat 2 is silent where it should be loud. The voice says "look at these words" and stops for 2 s; the five readings exist only as small text. Nobody reads five words and five transliterations in 7 s, and many viewers do not know how "bough" is said. Let the voice read them («դոու, թրու, թաֆ, քոֆ, բաու»). It is the same device as «քնիխտ», and it is the joke.

Structure and payoff. knight at 0:17-0:32 (the first payoff). The shift at 0:33-0:52. Printing and the "photograph" line at 0:52-1:05: the reveal, at about 1:03. Then a 50 s tail with three extensions: latin (19 s), armenian (15 s), reform (16 s). F133 asked for one.

  • F170 (must) The takeaway says «Ամեն տարօրինակ տառ ցույց է տալիս, թե ինչպես էին խոսում հինգ հարյուր տարի առաջ». Beat 6 has just shown two odd letters that were never spoken (the b in debt, the s in island). Either beat 6 goes (F171) or "every" does.
  • F171 (should) Cut beat 6 (latin), or keep one sentence of it. It is a second mechanism in a one-mechanism video, it breaks the takeaway, and it is the hardest beat by ear: the voice says «պարտք» and «կղզի» and the viewer must map them to "debt" and "island" on screen while hearing «դեբիտում» and «ինսուլա». If one thing stays, keep island as a joke (gloss: "one silent letter was even added by mistake").
  • F172 (must decide; on its face, from general knowledge, not checked) Beat 7 credits Mashtots for the fact that "today we read Armenian almost as written". Today's spelling in Armenia was reformed in the Soviet period; Western Armenian and the diaspora keep the classical spelling, where writing and speech match less. A diaspora viewer will say so in the first comment. And beat 8 then argues that a full reform is not practical, while Armenian is a case where one was made and is still argued about. Options: (a) claim less (gloss: "Mashtots built one letter per sound, and Armenian spelling has stayed close to speech"); (b) use it: it is the strongest local anchor this script could have and an honest answer to beat 8's own question. (b) is a sensitive topic; your call.
  • F173 (should) Beat 4 (shift) has three problems. The date range is spelled out («Մոտավորապես հազար չորս հարյուրից հազար յոթ հարյուր թվականներին»): 7 s of years before the verb, and two full lines as a caption; "over about three hundred years" with the dates on screen does the job. Three sound pairs in 8 s are six nonsense syllables, and old "bite" («բիթ») rhymes with today's "meet" («միթ»), so by ear they blur; one or two pairs are enough, and "house" («հաուս» / «հուս») is the clearest. The vowel trapezoid is a linguist's diagram that a phone viewer will not read. Simpler: two rows per word, a spelling row that never changes and a sound row (in Armenian letters) that slides. Beat 5's press then stamps the spelling row while the sound row keeps moving, and beats 4 and 5 become one continuous shot.
  • F174 [heard] Beat 3 order: "600 years ago they read it «քնիխտ». Today they say «նայթ». Then the sounds changed, and the writing did not." «Հետո» after «Այսօր» runs time backwards for a moment. Put the "then" sentence between the other two?
  • F175 (could) 1.5 s of silence after «Ստացվեց հին արտասանության լուսանկար», while the flash freezes the word. It is the line of the video and it has no air.

Ending. Lands, once F170 is fixed. The picture returns to knight and then "loops to the hook bars": two loops. With F168 the loop is the -ough words.

Pauses and visuals. One pause, on the right beat but used wrongly (F169). The knight letters lighting up one by one and the press stamping a frozen word are good pictures. The Caxton engraving is a real image nobody will recognise; it adds little.

Length. Fine. About 1:50-1:55 after F171, with the readings added in beat 2.

U questions, my pick. U-qq: keep the transliterations and add the five (F169). U-rr: not clear by ear (F171).

4. week_seven

8 beats, 1,619 characters, about 112 s of speech, about 1:57, 2.5 s of marked pauses.

Verdict: small fixes (order, and one gap in the showcase). The best first 3 seconds of the batch and the most ambitious showcase. The showcase is also the riskiest beat in the batch: arithmetic with remainders, by ear.

What works. The hook. The Moon objection is dealt with in 9 s. "Two places" is announced and then delivered as «Առաջինը», «Երկրորդը», so the listener always knows where he is. The Soviet week with spouses resting on different days is local, human and new to most viewers under 60.

Hook: strong. By 3.3 s the viewer has heard both sentences: «Օրը, ամիսը և տարին գալիս են երկնքից։ Շաբաթը՝ ոչ։» The other 8 s explain the three and ask the question.

  • F176 (could) Sentence 4 («Իսկ յոթ օրը ոչ մի բանի պտույտ չէ») repeats sentence 2. Without it the hook is under 9 s.

Structure and payoff. The answer (two sources) is complete at 0:46. The showcase runs 0:46-1:12, the Armenian day names 1:12-1:26, the failed reforms 1:26-1:47. So again three extensions after the answer.

  • F177 (should) Move beat 6 (armenian) up to right after beat 3 (sabbath). The Jewish count and the Armenian names are one thought: "hence our word «շաբաթ»" and "hence երկուշաբթի" belong together, and the Armenian viewer gets the personal reward at 0:27, not at 1:12. Then the planets come as "the other tradition, the one English and French kept", the order riddle is the one extension, and the Soviet week closes.
  • F178 (must) Beat 5 has a gap in its logic. It says each hour had a ruler, a day has 24 hours, 24 leaves 3, "so each next day starts three steps on". It never says that a day is named after the planet of its first hour. Without that half-sentence the conclusion does not follow. Also, by ear the question «Բայց ինչո՞ւ այս հերթով» has no "this": the voice has given only three days in order (Saturn, Sun, Moon), the other four are on screen in French. Say what is odd first (gloss: "Saturn, then the Sun, then the Moon: in the sky they do not stand in that order").

Leftovers of the old habits: «2020 թվականին հետազոտողները նկարագրեցին» (dmanisi beat 6), the Turing award sentence (zero_knowledge beat 6), «Մյունխենի հետազոտողները կրկնեցին փորձը» (ikea_effect beat 5), «հույն գրիչ Հռոփանոսի օգնությամբ» (mashtots beat 5). So yes, it worked. The hooks are the clearest gain: every one opens on its claim or its puzzle. The best local anchors are inside the idea (Armenia in the map showcase, prices in drams, the Armenian day names) and the weaker ones are an aside at the end (the pavement line, the Russian loanwords).

New patterns that repeat in this batch

  1. An eight-beat mold. The last eight scripts written all have exactly 8 beats and run 1:38-2:12: hook, two or three core beats, a "why", an extension, a doubt or "today" beat, a one-line close. decoy_popcorn and ikea_effect are the same skeleton beat for beat (beat 5 of both is a why that opens «Ինչո՞ւ է ...»). The old template was broken and a tidier one grew back.
  2. A doubt slot before the ending. dmanisi (beats 5-9), decoy_popcorn (7), ikea_effect (6), painted_statues (7), luggage_wheels (7), plus "we do not know" inside minute_60 (2) and english_spelling (4). Five of eleven say "we do not know" or "there is no clear answer". It works in decoy_popcorn and painted_statues, where the doubt is about the video's own claim and ends on something solid. It fails in three ways: it debunks a story the viewer had never heard (the cake mix, the Mead quote), it shrugs at a question the video itself asked (why so late; why sixty), or it stacks (dmanisi, seven in a row). See F223.
  3. "They tried to change it and failed" is the second-to-last beat of all three origin shorts: english_spelling (reform), week_seven (France and the USSR), minute_60 (France). The two time shorts even share the sentence «Ֆրանսիական հեղափոխությունը մտցրեց ...».
  4. "Not A, but B" as the closing line. dmanisi («Ոչ թե որովհետև ..., այլ որովհետև ...»), english_spelling («սխալ չէ՝ այն հին է»), week_seven («ոչ բնության օրենք է, ոչ մաթեմատիկա։ Դա ...»), map_projection («սուտ չի ասում, այլ ...»), painted_statues («ոչ թե հույներից է, այլ ...»), zero_knowledge («նույն բանը չեն»): 6 of 11, and three more close on a two-part contrast (mashtots, minute_60, ikea_effect). Three hooks use it too (english_spelling, decoy_popcorn, painted_statues). It is a better frame than «Այնպես որ, ... հարցրու», because it reframes. It is still one frame (F225).
  5. The closing screen line and the closing voice say different things in week_seven and map_projection, and the screen line is the tighter sentence in luggage_wheels, painted_statues and ikea_effect. The viewer reads one sentence while hearing another, with the captions as a third text (F226).
  6. Origin shorts turn into lists. The scripts that explain one mechanism (zero_knowledge, decoy_popcorn, map_projection) are tight. The ones that tell where something comes from (minute_60, week_seven, english_spelling, luggage_wheels, and mashtots in its second half) add one nice fact per beat: «Հետո», «Իսկ», «նաև». Pattern 4 of the first review (the long tail after the payoff) is alive there: three extensions each in english_spelling and week_seven (F224).
  7. Pauses are marked, small, and once not on the showcase. 1.5-3.5 s a script. mashtots has none on its showcase beat, map_projection has 1.5 s for a video that is all motion (F222).
  8. Twins. decoy_popcorn and ikea_effect (same skeleton, same author on the source line). week_seven and minute_60 (same hook frame, three normal things and then the exception, and the same France beat). mashtots and english_spelling are a deliberate pair, which is fine (F227).
  9. Words that tell the viewer how to feel. «հուզիչ պատմություն» (dmanisi), «ավելի ծիծաղելի էր» (ikea_effect), «տարօրինակ պատասխան ունի» (luggage_wheels), «ազնիվ վերապահում» and «ազնիվն այն է» (painted_statues, dmanisi), «Պատճառը պարզ է» and «Սկզբունքը պարզ էր» (map_projection, mashtots), «Գեղեցիկ պատմություն է, բայց» (ikea_effect). It is F128 again in new clothes (F228).
  10. Things only the screen says. The five -ough readings (english_spelling beat 2), the Latin behind «մինուտ» and «սեկունդ» (minute_60 beat 5), the four French day names that make the order odd (week_seven beat 4), "debt" and "island" behind «պարտք» and «կղզի». A listener who is not reading loses the point in each (F169, F185, F178, F171).
  11. A good habit, for the record: 16 of 93 beats open on a question, and most of them at a turn («Ինչո՞ւ չվերցնել պատրաստի հունական տառերը», «Իսկ ի՞նչ իմացավ նա», «Փոխել փորձե՞լ են»). The first review asked for exactly this. map_projection has none and could use one (F205).

Findings for the whole batch

  • F222 (should) Spend the silence. Budget 8-12 s a script, not 2, and put the longest pause on the showcase. Every script has 45-80 s of headroom. The second way to use the room is to let the viewer do something: choose a popcorn before the medium appears (F191), read five words aloud with the voice (F169), count twelve joints on his own hand (F189), watch one round as the colour-blind friend (F210). mashtots already does it with ճնճղուկ, and it is the best moment in the batch.
  • F223 (should) A rule for the doubt beat. Three conditions: it is about a claim this video made, it comes after one solid thing, and it ends on what is known. And never tell a story only to take it back. By this rule the cake-mix beat goes (F195), the Mead beat goes (F153), "why so late" gets asked once (F219), and dmanisi's seven doubts become one turn (F154).
  • F224 (should) For an origin short, find the one turn and make every beat serve it: "the sounds moved and print froze the letters" (english_spelling), "two traditions met" (week_seven), "sixty divides well" (minute_60), "a wheel is only as good as its floor" (luggage_wheels). A fact that does not serve the turn goes to the description or to its own short. And let the beat count follow the content, not the mold: ikea_effect is better at 7 (F195), dmanisi at 8 (F153, F154), map_projection at 9 with the split in F203.
  • F225 (could) Vary the closing frame. Keep "not A, but B" for the three or four where it is the idea (english_spelling, map_projection, painted_statues, dmanisi). The others have a choice ready: an instruction (decoy_popcorn already), an image (mashtots can close on «բայց գիրը մնաց»), a question back to the viewer.
  • F226 (should) One rule for the last beat: the voice says the screen line. Applies to week_seven (F181), map_projection (F202), luggage_wheels (F218), and loosely to painted_statues and ikea_effect.
  • F227 (should) Separate the twins. decoy_popcorn and ikea_effect: far apart in the order, and one of them out of the shared skeleton (ikea_effect changes anyway with F195). week_seven and minute_60: remove the France sentence from one (F182), or re-spine minute_60 around the clock (F186), and do not publish them back to back.
  • F228 (could) [ear] Cut the words that announce a feeling (pattern 9). The frog is funny without «ծիծաղելի», and the caveat is honest without «ազնիվ».
  • F229 (should decide) Digits or words. Five scripts write digits («405», «63 տոկոսով», «2018»), six write numbers out («հազար ութ հարյուր դրամ», «ութսունչորս տոկոսն»). The captions come from the same text, so a spelled-out number becomes a long caption («հազար չորս հարյուրից հազար յոթ հարյուր թվականներին» is two lines) where digits would be one glance. The timeline now weights digits by their spoken length, so digits no longer hurt the timing. One convention for the series; my pick is digits in the text, checked in the TTS test (F230).
  • F230 (should) One cheap TTS test call before anything in this batch is voiced, with the risky items: the classical sentence and the letter names (mashtots), the transliterated English («քնիխտ», «դոու, թրու, թաֆ, քոֆ, բաու», «բայթ», «հաուս»), the names («Հռոփանոս», «Սեջոն», «Սեքվոյա», «Մերկատոր», «Գաուս», «Թյուրինգ»), «երեսուներեք ամբողջ երեք, երեք, երեք», and digits with endings («63 տոկոսով», «405 թվականին»).
  • F231 (must before voicing; not a fact check, only the plans' own flags) Four plans still carry "check before voicing" on facts that are in the script: mashtots (Hangul's mouth shapes, Sequoyah), week_seven («նախօրյակ» for ուրբաթ), minute_60 (the rising star groups), zero_knowledge (the origin and the Turing award). A fifth, english_spelling, marks Webster as "from general knowledge". Three of these shrink or go away with the cuts above (F162, F183, F208).
  • F232 (could; a decision about the channel) The README says "shorts about thinking errors". Of these eleven, four fit that promise (decoy_popcorn, ikea_effect, dmanisi, painted_statues) and map_projection fits through its close. The other six are "why is this everyday thing the way it is" (mashtots, english_spelling, week_seven, minute_60, luggage_wheels) or an explainer (zero_knowledge). Both kinds are good. If the channel now has two strands, say so in the README and alternate them in the order (F233 does).

Tone and consistency

  • The tone moved back towards the pilot, which F124 asked about. There are jokes again, about one a script, and they are dry: the suitcase on a strap «շան պես», Africa «ինչպես հալած պանիր», grandfather's fence, the Yerevan pavements. The voice talks to the viewer: «Փորձիր» (twice), «Նայիր», «Պատկերացրու», «Ասենք». The written-language words of the first batch are nearly gone (one «կիրառվում է», one «մինչդեռ»).
  • «գրեթե» won (F126): nine uses, against one «համարյա» (dmanisi beat 3, F160).

  • Address is «դու» everywhere, as before.
  • Source lines mix Armenian («Կորյուն, «Վարք Մաշտոցի»», «Բրիտանական թանգարան, 1937-1938») and English citations. That is fine: papers in Latin letters, everything else in Armenian.

This batch against the first one

  • Better: hooks (no weak openings left), length discipline, far fewer numbers (bayes_test alone had about 40 number mentions), no summary cards, earlier payoffs, local anchors, sentences that are easier to follow by ear, and four scripts I would voice as they are (the first batch had none).
  • Worse or new: the batch leans on "nice fact" beats, where the first one leaned on demonstrations. bayes_test, friendship and simpson each show one mechanism at work for a minute; here only decoy_popcorn, zero_knowledge and map_projection do that. The origin shorts are pleasant and less likely to be remembered a week later. And the doubt beat became a reflex.
  • Same: honesty labelling is done with care everywhere («վարկած», «օրինակ, գները հորինված են», «Ասում են»). The two gaps are both pictures: the paleo-art (F157) and the reconstructions (F212).

The three strongest

  1. decoy_popcorn. The best hook, drams, a mechanism in plain words, the doubt beat done right, a close the viewer can use tonight. Ready.
  2. map_projection. Proof on screen at 6 seconds, Armenia inside the showcase, an ending that turns the story around. The most work to animate and the most rewatchable.
  3. painted_statues. A flat claim in 2.4 s, facts that climb, real photos, cheap to make. It needs its best pictures up front (F211).

A close fourth is zero_knowledge: the cleanest explanation in the batch, for a narrower audience. And mashtots has the highest ceiling with Armenian viewers if the hook and the order are fixed.

The two weakest

  1. luggage_wheels. Two half-videos and a takeaway that belongs to one of them. It needs a decision, not polish (F216).
  2. minute_60. Eight beats, eight facts. The good short inside it is "sixty divides well, and a third of a hundred never ends" (F183, F184, F186).

dmanisi is not on this list, although it has the longest stretch of doubt: its idea is single and its close is the best in either batch. It needs two cuts.
