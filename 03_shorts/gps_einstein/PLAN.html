The idea in one paragraph

GPS finds position by timing satellite signals, but orbital clocks and Earth-reference clocks do not run at the same rate. The showcase combines rounded daily contributions: motion about -7 microseconds, gravitational potential about +45, net about +38 for an unadjusted clock. The doubt beat then asks exactly what this permits us to infer: clock error is not a universal position-error trajectory, because receivers estimate their clock bias alongside position. Four satellites supply the basic four-unknown solution, and real noncircular orbits need additional time corrections. Armenia's documented reference-station network connects satellite measurement to a local use without inventing its performance or attributing the relativity correction to it.

Facts (checked)

Evidence base: _knowledge/short_videos/facts_codex_4_technology_2026-11-28.md, I1. All used sheet claims are high confidence. Ashby, NIST, Misra and the Cadastre Committee page were also reopened online during drafting. The microsecond definition was additionally verified directly in NIST's explanation.

  • Timing and ranging (hook, travel_time): departure/arrival timing and signal propagation allow distance equations and a simultaneous position/time solution. «Ժամանակից կարելի է հաշվել հեռավորությունը» is the conceptual relation; it does not claim an inexpensive unsynchronised receiver directly measures exact ranges without estimating its own clock bias. Neil Ashby, 2003, Introduction and section 6.
  • Motion contribution - rounded (motion): for nominal GPS conditions, approximately -7 microseconds/day relative to Earth-reference clocks. NIST, Putting Einstein to the Test, 2025.
  • Microsecond definition - additional direct check (motion): NIST explicitly explains microseconds as millionths of a second. This is a unit definition, not an additional observed GPS error. NIST, opened 2026-11-28.
  • Gravitational contribution and net - rounded (gravity, clock_showcase): the difference in gravitational potential between orbit and Earth reference gives about +45 microseconds/day; together with the motion contribution the unadjusted clock gains about +38 microseconds/day. The phrase «ձգողության պայմանների տարբերությունը» is the plain-language rendering of potential difference, not a statement that there is no gravity in orbit. NIST, Ashby, section 5.
  • Clock error versus position error (doubt): the receiver estimates its own clock bias. In Misra's explicitly idealised identical circular-orbit case, the common satellite-clock bias is absorbed in that estimate without moving the position estimate. This does not remove all real relativistic corrections. Pratap Misra, 2023, original engineering analysis. The narration limits this to a common component that can enter the clock estimate; the diagram is labelled idealised. Neither the familiar 10 km/day claim nor a substitute 11 km/day position claim is used.
  • Basic receiver solution (receiver): measurements from 4 satellites determine 3 position coordinates and 1 receiver clock bias. Ashby, section 6. This is the basic solution, not a claim that every receiver uses exactly four satellites at all times.
  • Real-orbit correction (receiver): eccentricity introduces an additional periodic relativistic correction. IS-GPS-200N, 2022, section 20.3.3.3.3.1, ESA Navipedia, Sanz Subirana, Juan Zornoza and Hernández-Pajares, 2011. The script supplies no universal amplitude, failure countdown or claim that a factory offset handles everything.
  • Armenian anchor (armenia): the Cadastre Committee describes a national satellite geodetic network and real-time reference stations created for the entire territory in 2013. The page is dated 2026; 2013 is the network creation year on the source line. Cadastre Committee, Geodesy. No accuracy number, station location, GPS-only equipment claim, or present operational-status guarantee is added.
  • Medium-or-lower claims used: none. No exact oscillator-frequency example, generic factory-adjustment claim, clock-to-kilometre conversion, or receiver failure time is voiced or animated.
Animation plan (showcase)
  • One showcase, clock_showcase: frame 0 already contains the two clock-offset bars. Over a notional day, combine the rounded -7 and +45 microseconds/day contributions and expose the rounded net +38. Keep «կլորացված թվեր» and the Earth-reference comparison visible. Hold for the 4 s pause so the viewer can compare the opposing bars and read the sum. The orbital clock is explicitly unadjusted. Do not animate a location marker travelling kilometres, or make a real-time clock hand jump by an invented visible amount.
  • Supporting beats: the hook begins with a location point, signal and clocks. travel_time shows schematic propagation paths. motion and gravity build the showcase's ingredients with no invented orbital altitude, speed or absolute gravitational value. The gravitational-potential shading is qualitative.
  • Doubt extension: keep position and receiver-clock estimate separate. A common timing component shifts the latter in a diagram labelled «Իդեալականացված օրինակ». During the 2 s pause let the viewer inspect the separate outputs. The following four-satellite diagram has 3 coordinate slots and 1 clock-bias slot; a noncircular orbit indicates an additional qualitative periodic correction without an invented numerical amplitude.
  • Local scene and close: use an outline of Armenia and schematic reference-station symbols, explicitly labelled as a scheme, never claimed station coordinates. Its 2 s pause allows reading the source line. Return to the stationary phone point with time still advancing; voice and closing screen line match. No summary card.
