The idea in one paragraph

Thursday October 4 turns into Friday October 15 in the first Gregorian-adopting jurisdictions. The opening makes that concrete puzzle visible at once; one continuous-day ribbon settles the date/time distinction without a second spoken recap. The missing cause now has scale: Julian excess is about 11 minutes per year, roughly one day in 128 years. The reform's ten-day adjustment has its own Nicaea reference, rather than being presented as an exact multiplication of a modern rounded rate. The leap-year rule and a viewer choice between 1900 and 2000 show the ongoing repair. The single doubt asks whether the new rule is perfect, ending with its much slower approximate 26-second annual excess. The local extension retains the Church's 1923 adoption, Jerusalem's exception and the present January 6/19 correspondence for Nativity and Theophany.

  • NEW: numerical Julian drift (drift): Julian average 365.25 days versus a modern approximate tropical year of 365.2422 days. Their rounded difference, 0.0078 day, gives approximately 11 minutes 14 seconds/year and one day in 128 years. Speech rounds to about 11 minutes; these are calculated comparisons, not recovered historical measurements. USNO, Leap Years.
  • NEW: why the reform corrected ten days (drift, including source line): the bull refers to about ten days' displacement since the Council of Nicaea. This is not all accumulated Julian error since Caesar. Inter gravissimas.
  • Gregorian rule and century examples (new_rule, centuries, doubt): divisible by 4, except century years must also be divisible by 400; 1900 not leap, 2000 leap. USNO.
  • NEW: approximate residual Gregorian excess (doubt): 365.2425 versus 365.2422 days is calculated as about 26 seconds/year. It establishes improvement without perfection; no inevitable future correction date is asserted. USNO.
  • Church adoption year (armenian_calendar): 1923 for the Armenian Church, with no independently checked decree day or civil transition claim. UK and Ireland Diocese, Our Faith.
  • Jerusalem exception (armenian_calendar): Jerusalem retains the Julian calendar. Official Western European church calendar explanation.
