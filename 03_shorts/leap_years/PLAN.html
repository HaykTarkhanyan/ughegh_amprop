The idea in one paragraph

Ten date labels were omitted where the Gregorian reform first took effect: Thursday 4 October was followed by Friday 15 October 1582. The continuous clock and weekday sequence make the distinction visible before the doubt beat asks whether anyone lost lived time. The cause is a small difference between the Julian calendar's average year and the seasonal year; the repair includes a different leap-year rule, illustrated by 1900 and 2000. The single extension is local: the Armenian Apostolic Church's 1923 adoption, Jerusalem's Julian exception, and the present January 6/19 correspondence. This concerns the combined Nativity and Theophany feast.

Facts (checked)

Evidence base: _knowledge/short_videos/facts_codex_3_physics_earth_2026-11-28.md, C5. All used sheet claims are high confidence. Facts below also cover the screen labels and animation. No new historical reconstruction or quote is asserted.

  • The skipped dates and uninterrupted weekdays (hook, calendar_jump, doubt): Thursday 4 October was followed by Friday 15 October 1582, in the initial adopting jurisdictions. Ten intervening date labels were omitted; this was not a worldwide simultaneous change or a loss of lived time. USNO, Julian Date explanatory notes, Gregory XIII, Inter gravissimas, 1582.
  • Year lengths and drift (drift): the Julian average is 365.25 days; a modern approximate mean tropical year is 365.2422 days. The narration uses the plain phrase «եղանակների շրջանը» for the latter. These are a calendar average and an astronomical approximation, not two exact universal constants. USNO, Leap Years. The sheet's checked values are the evidence here; the live USNO FAQ timed out during this drafting pass. No exact historical equinox displacement is calculated from those rounded values.
  • Gregorian rule and examples (new_rule, centuries): years divisible by 4 are leap years except centuries must also be divisible by 400. Therefore 1900 was not leap and 2000 was. The reform improves seasonal alignment without making it perfect. USNO, Leap Years.
  • Church adoption, not civil adoption (armenian_calendar): the official diocesan explanation supports 1923 for the Armenian Church's adoption of the Gregorian calendar; it does not establish a precise decree day or Armenia's civil-calendar transition. UK and Ireland Diocese, Our Faith.
  • Jerusalem's exception (armenian_calendar): the Jerusalem See retains the Julian calendar. Armenian Apostolic Church, Western European legation, church holidays.
  • The combined feast and present conversion (christmas): Etchmiadzin celebrates Holy Nativity and Theophany on January 6. In the present calendar relationship, Jerusalem's January 6 Julian corresponds to January 19 Gregorian. The 2026 Jerusalem itinerary distinguishes the eve and feast on January 18/19; only the feast day is voiced. Mother See, January 6, 2026, Eastern Diocese, 2026 Holy Land itinerary. «Հիմա» deliberately limits the correspondence; it is not permanently a 13-day difference.
  • Scope exclusions: no British riot story, no perfect-calendar claim, no December 25 explanation for Armenian January 6, no precise 1923 decree date. Every voiced year is part of the calendar comparison itself; researchers and publication years stay on source lines.
