## The idea in one paragraph

The box you assembled is worth more to you than the same box ready-made, and it has been measured in money:
people who built a simple box would pay 63% more for it than people handed the same box built. The boxes were from
IKEA, hence the name. Amateur origami makers valued their crumpled frogs almost like experts' work, while onlookers
saw nearly nothing. The catch: you have to finish; build-then-destroy or half-built kills the effect. Why: a
finished thing becomes a little part of you, no longer a box but your box (a Munich replication found the same,
through psychological ownership). The same pull works wherever our effort is: a business plan, code, grandfather's
crooked fence. The takeaway: it really is great, for you; to others it is just a box. (After the Fable review: the
cake-mix legend is cut, the effect is named, one object from hook to close.)

## Facts (checked)

- **The study:** Norton, Mochon & Ariely, "The IKEA effect: When labor leads to love", *Journal of Consumer
  Psychology* 22 (2012; working paper 2011): IKEA storage boxes, origami and Lego; builders were willing to pay 63%
  more for their own assembled box (Wikipedia, IKEA effect; Harvard DASH copy). The effect "dissipated" when
  participants built and then destroyed their creation, or did not complete it.
- **Origami:** amateurs valued their own creations nearly as highly as experts' origami, while others valued them
  little (the paper's qualitative result; the exact cent values were not checked, so the script gives no numbers).
- **Replication:** Sarstedt, Neubert & Barth, "The IKEA effect: A conceptual replication", *Journal of Marketing
  Behavior* 2017 (LMU Munich's repository holds it): supports the effect and points to psychological ownership.
- **Competence:** Mochon, Norton & Ariely 2014 ("Bolstering and restoring feelings of competence via the IKEA
  effect") is the basis for «ապացույց, որ կարող ենք».
- **Cake mix legend:** widely told (Dichter, General Mills removing dried egg); food historians call most of it a
  myth: Duff's mixes with dried egg sold well, and Pillsbury kept a complete mix and still thrived (Culinary Lore,
  MNopedia, Future Commerce). Told with «Հաճախ պատմում են» and «պատմաբանները կասկածում են».
- **Ideas and code:** "not invented here" is a known pattern; the script says «միտում», not a measured effect.
- **Ariely context:** see the decoy plan (a 2012 paper he co-authored was retracted in 2021); the IKEA effect stands
  on an independent replication, so the source line can keep the original paper.
