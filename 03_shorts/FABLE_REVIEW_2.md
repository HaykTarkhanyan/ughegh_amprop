## 0. How to read this, and the length table

- Every item you may act on has an ID, **F153 to F238**, continuing the first review. Answer by ID.
- Each item is tagged **must** (fix or decide before the voice is made), **should** (makes the video clearly
  better) or **could** (optional).
- Armenian items are questions, never rewrites. **[heard]** = structural, about following it by ear (sentence
  length, ambiguity): fairly reliable. **[ear]** = "sounds off to me": right about 1 time in 9, so there are very
  few. "Checkable" = a repeated word, an unclear referent, a mismatch between two beats.
- "Gloss" = an English version of a possible line. The Armenian wording is yours.
- Lengths are the length lines in each `PLAN.md` (`tools/plan_script.py`), not recounted. Beat start times were
  computed with the same rate (870 characters a minute, pauses included) only to say at what second a payoff lands.
- Verdicts are about the script. **ready** = can be voiced as written, the findings are improvements.
  **small fixes** = do the must and should items first. **rework** = the structure needs a decision.
- Each section ends with my pick on that plan's open U questions.

## 1. dmanisi

10 beats, 1,903 characters, about 132 s of speech, about 2:14. No marked pauses.

**Verdict: small fixes (two cuts).** One idea, the channel's own, and the best closing line of all 22 scripts.
The problem is proportion: the story gets 16 s and the doubt gets 75 s.

**What works.** A real skull in frame 0. The "how do we know" beat (sockets fill with bone, and that takes years)
comes before the story, so the viewer trusts the fact before he hears the interpretation. The neighbour anchor in
beat 2 is natural, not bolted on. The three short sentences in beat 5 («Բայց ի՞նչ է ասում ինքը՝ ոսկորը։ Այն ասում է,
որ նա ապրել է առանց ատամների։ Այն չի ասում՝ ինչպես։») are the turn of the video and need nothing.

**Hook: strong.** By 3 s the viewer has "almost two million years ago a man lost" plus the skull; the teeth land
at about 4 s and the question at 8 s. The first sentence is 5.2 s, but it is the claim, not a lead-in. One risk:
«Ինչպե՞ս» promises an answer and the video ends on "we do not know which" (the yawning problem, F112). Here it
mostly works, because the viewer guesses "someone fed him", has that confirmed at 0:32 and is turned around at
0:49. Keep it.

**Structure and payoff.** The story lands at 0:32-0:48, the turn comes at 0:49. Then 75 s that all say "not so
sure": the bone does not say how; the authors raised both readings; a bonobo managed; the Mead quote is
unverified; Neanderthal care is disputed; one bone cannot tell; we do not know which. Seven statements of doubt
in a row. It is pattern 8 of the first review (honesty becoming the content), and it survives only because here
the doubt is the point. It should be one clean turn and one piece of evidence, not five.

- **F153** (should) Cut beat 7 (mead, 19 s, the longest beat). It brings in a person and a quote and then takes
  the quote back. If the viewer has never seen the quote, he gains nothing (the qwerty-hook fault). If he has, it
  is a second debunk inside a video that has been debunking for 30 s. It is also a different lesson (a
  misattributed quote) from this video's (reading too much into evidence). The quote is material for its own
  short. Question: has your audience seen the femur quote (it circulates in Russian)? If yes and you want it, one
  sentence, not three.
- **F154** (should) Cut beat 8 (still), or fold one clause of it into beat 9. It asks "so is compassion made
  up?", answers "no", and then backs the "no" with evidence it calls disputed in the same sentence: claim and undo
  in one breath (kids_tv beats 7-9 did this). Beat 9 already guards against the wrong conclusion («Երկու
  պատմությունն էլ ուշագրավ են»). Also beats 8, 9 and 10 each end on a closing line ("one bone does not tell us
  everything" / "the honest thing is to say we do not know which" / the takeaway): three landings in a row. With
  F153 and F154 the order is hook, find, sockets, story, bone_says, bonobo, both, takeaway.
- **F155** (should) Mark pauses; there are none. The takeaway says we want beautiful stories to be true, so the
  viewer has to want it first: 2-2.5 s on the painting after beat 4, before «Բայց». Then 1 s after «Այն չի ասում՝
  ինչպես», and 1.5 s after «չգիտենք որը». About 5 s in all.
- **F156** (could) Beat 6 opens with «2020 թվականին հետազոտողները նկարագրեցին»: a year and "researchers described"
  as the first words, the bibliography opener of the first batch (patterns 5 and 6). The source line has both.
  Open on the animal.

**Ending.** Lands, and it is earned. «Ոչ թե որովհետև դրանք սխալ են, այլ որովհետև շատ ենք ուզում, որ ճիշտ լինեն» is
the sentence a viewer will repeat. It needs the pause before it (F155) and one landing before it, not three (F154).

**Pauses and visuals.** Mostly stills and cards; the only moving demonstration is the sockets filling with bone.

- **F157** (must; the channel's own honesty rule) The most persuasive picture in the video is a painting of the
  story that the video then questions. Tag it on screen as an artist's picture (gloss: "an artist's
  reconstruction"), the way the pilot's red-dot plane carries «նկարազարդում». The same for the two drawn scenes in
  beat 9.
- **F158** (could) Give the second half one picture that stays, instead of five cards (two labels, a bonobo, a
  quote card, a skeleton with a tipping scale, two scenes). Keep the skull on screen from beat 5 with two columns:
  "the bone says" (one solid line that never grows) and "we add" (the stories, dashed). Each new item lands in a
  column, and the viewer sees the takeaway before he hears it.

**Length.** Fine. About 1:45-1:50 after the two cuts and the pauses.

**Armenian (questions).**

- **F159** [heard] Beat 5, last sentence: 157 characters, about 11 s, two clauses joined by «բայց նաև» and an
  example at the end. Two sentences?
- **F160** (checkable) «Գրեթե» in beat 1 and «համարյա» in beat 3 (F126; the other ten scripts of this batch use
  only «գրեթե»). «ազգական» has two referents: «մեր հեռավոր ազգականն էր» (the viewer's kin, beat 2) and «ծերացած
  ազգականի մասին» (the helper's kin, beat 4). «կարեկցանք» means compassion here and carried "empathy" in yawning
  (F119): one answer decides both.

**U questions, my pick.** U-ii: keep the honest angle, shorter (F153, F154). U-kk: yes, credit the video in the
description.

## 2. mashtots

10 beats, 1,806 characters, about 125 s of speech, about 2:11, 3.5 s of marked pauses.

**Verdict: small fixes (order).** The highest ceiling in the batch for this audience: an identity topic with
three things most viewers were not taught at school (why Greek letters would not do, the Greek order that was
kept, the letters as numbers). Two risks: the first 5 seconds ("I know this") and an ending that peaks one beat
too early.

**What works.** «Փորձիր հունական տառերով գրել «ճնճղուկ» բառը» with a pause is the best single line in the batch:
the viewer runs the experiment himself. The letter names said aloud (այբ, բեն, գիմ, դա next to ալֆա, բետա, գամմա,
դելտա) make the borrowing audible with no screen at all. «Թագավորությունը շուտով կորավ, բայց գիրը մնաց» is the
emotional peak. The hook's "even a first sentence" is paid at 1:19.

**Hook: ok.** By 3 s the viewer has the whole first sentence ("most alphabets have no author"). "Ours has one"
lands at about 9 s and the question at 12.8 s: the longest hook in the batch, four sentences.

- **F161** (should) Two things. (a) The closing question ("why would a person sit down and invent letters?") has
  a school answer every Armenian viewer already knows; the fresh material is the how. Promise that. (b) «զրոյից»
  oversells: beat 5 shows he kept the Greek order and built on it. That is the most interesting beat and the hook
  denies it in advance. Two ways out: promise the how (gloss: "Ours has an author, a year and a first sentence.
  And he did not start from nothing: here is what he kept from the Greeks and what he had to invent."), or open on
  the ճնճղուկ challenge itself as frame 0 and second 0, since it is this script's own unique hook, and give the
  "author, year, first sentence" line right after. Either way, under 9 s.

**Structure and payoff.** School history at 0:13-0:28 (the part viewers know, and the slowest stretch). ճնճղուկ at
0:28-0:41. The showcase (the two rows) at 0:49-1:11. The first sentence at 1:19. The peak line at 1:45. Then
Korea and the Cherokee at 1:46-2:02, then the takeaway.

- **F162** (should) Beat 9 (others) sits between the peak line and the takeaway and lets the air out. It also
  does not do what the plan says ("others did the same for the same reason"): the beat gives no reason, only two
  unrelated curiosities with two names nobody will keep. My pick: cut it (about 1:55 left) and end result ->
  takeaway; Hangul's mouth-shaped letters deserve their own short, not 8 seconds. Second choice: keep one of the
  two and move it up to right after beat 5, so the video still ends at home (U-nn).
- **F163** (should) Beat 5 (design) is the showcase and the densest beat: 21.5 s with a year, a region, a
  helper's name, a count, the principle and eight letter names, and no pause. Move «հույն գրիչ Հռոփանոսի
  օգնությամբ» and the cities to the source line, split the beat after «մեկ հնչյուն, մեկ տառ», and mark 2 s after the
  Greek-order sentence so the rows can link. The year 405 can stay: every viewer knows it and the hook promised it.
- **F164** (must before voicing; one cheap TTS call) Test what the voice may mangle: the classical sentence in
  beat 7 (U-mm), the letter names in beats 3 and 5 («ծա, ձա, ճե, ջե, ղատ, ռա»; «այբ, բեն, գիմ, դա»), and «Հռոփանոս»,
  «Սեջոն», «Սեքվոյա» if they stay. If the classical line comes out wrong, voice only the modern version and show
  the classical one written.
- **F165** (should; on its face, not checked) Frame 0 shows Latin, Greek and Cyrillic with "?" as the author.
  Viewers who went to a post-Soviet school "know" that Cyrillic has named authors and will say so. Use alphabets
  nobody attributes to a person, or drop Cyrillic from the frame.
- **F166** (could) Two short side beats. Beat 4 (daniel, 8 s) is a second "not enough" right after ճնճղուկ; it can
  go, or stay as the one detail viewers remember from school. Beat 6 (numbers, 8 s) is a side fact as written. It
  becomes part of the design if the picture is the 36 letters as a 4 x 9 grid (units, tens, hundreds,
  thousands), which also shows why 36 is a tidy number. The grid is from general knowledge: check before use.

**Ending.** Lands. The takeaway repeats the hook's first two sentences almost word for word, which is fine as a
loop, and «Մերը որոշում էր» is repeatable. It is weaker than the line before beat 9; after F162 the two sit next
to each other and carry each other.

**Pauses and visuals.** Both pauses are on the right beats (the viewer tries ճնճղուկ; the first sentence is
written out). The showcase has none (F163). The Greek and Armenian rows with the new letters dropping in are the
picture of this video and will be worth the work.

**Length.** Fine at 2:11; about 1:55-2:00 after F162 with the extra pause.

**Armenian (questions).**

- **F167** (checkable) Beat 2 moves from the present («բաժանված է», «կարդում են», «չեն հասկանում») to the past
  («քարոզում էր», «տեսնում էր») inside one beat. Intended?

**U questions, my pick.** U-mm: both versions if the test passes (F164). U-nn: cut, or one example moved up
(F162). U-oo: leave the Georgian claim out, agreed.

## 3. english_spelling

9 beats, 1,746 characters, about 120 s of speech, about 2:05, 2 s of marked pauses.

**Verdict: small fixes.** A clear, repeatable idea, the best image in words in the batch («հին արտասանության
լուսանկար») and a takeaway people will quote («սխալ չէ՝ այն հին է»). Two musts: the takeaway contradicts beat 6, and
the claim about Armenian will be challenged.

**What works.** «քնիխտ» said aloud. "Printing arrived exactly while the sounds were moving" is a mechanism, not a
list of facts. Keeping English out of the voice works: the transliterations are fun to hear.

**Hook: ok.** By 3 s the viewer has "English children learn to read two..."; the comparison lands at 6.5 s (the
longest first sentence in the batch), and the second sentence gives the cause. So by 0:10 the hook has asked and
answered its own question. The open question (why is the spelling like this?) is never said.

- **F168** (should) Open on beat 2. The five -ough words are the better frame 0 and the better first 3 seconds:
  every viewer who learned English at school has suffered from them, while the reading speed of Scottish
  first-graders is far from him. Then the statistic as the consequence (gloss: "that is why English children need
  twice as long to learn to read"), then «Բայց մի ժամանակ ...».
- **F169** (should) Beat 2 is silent where it should be loud. The voice says "look at these words" and stops for
  2 s; the five readings exist only as small text. Nobody reads five words and five transliterations in 7 s, and
  many viewers do not know how "bough" is said. Let the voice read them («դոու, թրու, թաֆ, քոֆ, բաու»). It is the
  same device as «քնիխտ», and it is the joke.

**Structure and payoff.** knight at 0:17-0:32 (the first payoff). The shift at 0:33-0:52. Printing and the
"photograph" line at 0:52-1:05: the reveal, at about 1:03. Then a 50 s tail with three extensions: latin (19 s),
armenian (15 s), reform (16 s). F133 asked for one.

- **F170** (must) The takeaway says «Ամեն տարօրինակ տառ ցույց է տալիս, թե ինչպես էին խոսում հինգ հարյուր տարի
  առաջ». Beat 6 has just shown two odd letters that were never spoken (the b in debt, the s in island). Either
  beat 6 goes (F171) or "every" does.
- **F171** (should) Cut beat 6 (latin), or keep one sentence of it. It is a second mechanism in a one-mechanism
  video, it breaks the takeaway, and it is the hardest beat by ear: the voice says «պարտք» and «կղզի» and the
  viewer must map them to "debt" and "island" on screen while hearing «դեբիտում» and «ինսուլա». If one thing
  stays, keep island as a joke (gloss: "one silent letter was even added by mistake").
- **F172** (must decide; on its face, from general knowledge, not checked) Beat 7 credits Mashtots for the fact
  that "today we read Armenian almost as written". Today's spelling in Armenia was reformed in the Soviet period;
  Western Armenian and the diaspora keep the classical spelling, where writing and speech match less. A diaspora
  viewer will say so in the first comment. And beat 8 then argues that a full reform is not practical, while
  Armenian is a case where one was made and is still argued about. Options: (a) claim less (gloss: "Mashtots
  built one letter per sound, and Armenian spelling has stayed close to speech"); (b) use it: it is the strongest
  local anchor this script could have and an honest answer to beat 8's own question. (b) is a sensitive topic;
  your call.
- **F173** (should) Beat 4 (shift) has three problems. The date range is spelled out («Մոտավորապես հազար չորս
  հարյուրից հազար յոթ հարյուր թվականներին»): 7 s of years before the verb, and two full lines as a caption; "over
  about three hundred years" with the dates on screen does the job. Three sound pairs in 8 s are six nonsense
  syllables, and old "bite" («բիթ») rhymes with today's "meet" («միթ»), so by ear they blur; one or two pairs are
  enough, and "house" («հաուս» / «հուս») is the clearest. The vowel trapezoid is a linguist's diagram that a phone
  viewer will not read. Simpler: two rows per word, a spelling row that never changes and a sound row (in
  Armenian letters) that slides. Beat 5's press then stamps the spelling row while the sound row keeps moving,
  and beats 4 and 5 become one continuous shot.
- **F174** [heard] Beat 3 order: "600 years ago they read it «քնիխտ». Today they say «նայթ». Then the sounds
  changed, and the writing did not." «Հետո» after «Այսօր» runs time backwards for a moment. Put the "then"
  sentence between the other two?
- **F175** (could) 1.5 s of silence after «Ստացվեց հին արտասանության լուսանկար», while the flash freezes the word.
  It is the line of the video and it has no air.

**Ending.** Lands, once F170 is fixed. The picture returns to knight and then "loops to the hook bars": two loops.
With F168 the loop is the -ough words.

**Pauses and visuals.** One pause, on the right beat but used wrongly (F169). The knight letters lighting up one
by one and the press stamping a frozen word are good pictures. The Caxton engraving is a real image nobody will
recognise; it adds little.

**Length.** Fine. About 1:50-1:55 after F171, with the readings added in beat 2.

**U questions, my pick.** U-qq: keep the transliterations and add the five (F169). U-rr: not clear by ear (F171).

## 4. week_seven

8 beats, 1,619 characters, about 112 s of speech, about 1:57, 2.5 s of marked pauses.

**Verdict: small fixes (order, and one gap in the showcase).** The best first 3 seconds of the batch and the most
ambitious showcase. The showcase is also the riskiest beat in the batch: arithmetic with remainders, by ear.

**What works.** The hook. The Moon objection is dealt with in 9 s. "Two places" is announced and then delivered
as «Առաջինը», «Երկրորդը», so the listener always knows where he is. The Soviet week with spouses resting on
different days is local, human and new to most viewers under 60.

### New patterns that repeat in this batch

1. **An eight-beat mold.** The last eight scripts written all have exactly 8 beats and run 1:38-2:12: hook, two or
   three core beats, a "why", an extension, a doubt or "today" beat, a one-line close. decoy_popcorn and
   ikea_effect are the same skeleton beat for beat (beat 5 of both is a `why` that opens «Ինչո՞ւ է ...»). The old
   template was broken and a tidier one grew back.
2. **A doubt slot before the ending.** dmanisi (beats 5-9), decoy_popcorn (7), ikea_effect (6), painted_statues
   (7), luggage_wheels (7), plus "we do not know" inside minute_60 (2) and english_spelling (4). Five of eleven say
   "we do not know" or "there is no clear answer". It works in decoy_popcorn and painted_statues, where the doubt
   is about the video's own claim and ends on something solid. It fails in three ways: it debunks a story the
   viewer had never heard (the cake mix, the Mead quote), it shrugs at a question the video itself asked (why so
   late; why sixty), or it stacks (dmanisi, seven in a row). See F223.
3. **"They tried to change it and failed"** is the second-to-last beat of all three origin shorts:
   english_spelling (reform), week_seven (France and the USSR), minute_60 (France). The two time shorts even share
   the sentence «Ֆրանսիական հեղափոխությունը մտցրեց ...».
4. **"Not A, but B" as the closing line.** dmanisi («Ոչ թե որովհետև ..., այլ որովհետև ...»), english_spelling («սխալ
   չէ՝ այն հին է»), week_seven («ոչ բնության օրենք է, ոչ մաթեմատիկա։ Դա ...»), map_projection («սուտ չի ասում, այլ
   ...»), painted_statues («ոչ թե հույներից է, այլ ...»), zero_knowledge («նույն բանը չեն»): 6 of 11, and three more
   close on a two-part contrast (mashtots, minute_60, ikea_effect). Three hooks use it too (english_spelling,
   decoy_popcorn, painted_statues). It is a better frame than «Այնպես որ, ... հարցրու», because it reframes. It is
   still one frame (F225).
5. **The closing screen line and the closing voice say different things** in week_seven and map_projection, and
   the screen line is the tighter sentence in luggage_wheels, painted_statues and ikea_effect. The viewer reads
   one sentence while hearing another, with the captions as a third text (F226).
