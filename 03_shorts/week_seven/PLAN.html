The idea in one paragraph

The day, the month and the year come from the sky; the week does not, and the Moon is not the answer (29.5 days does not split into sevens). Seven comes from two traditions. The first is the Jewish Sabbath week, which Armenian kept: the week starts on Sunday, so «երկուշաբթի» is literally the week's second day. The second is the Roman planetary week, one day for each of the seven moving lights, which English kept (Saturday, Sunday, Monday). Their odd order (Saturn, then the Sun, then the Moon) has a neat cause: the hours had ruling planets in slow-to-fast order, a day was named after the planet of its first hour, and 24 leaves 3 when divided by 7, so each day starts three planets on, which draws a seven-pointed star. The Soviet five- and six-day weeks (Armenia included, spouses with different days off) failed. The takeaway: the week is not from the sky but from people. (After the Fable review: the Armenian names moved up, the missing "named after the first hour" link added, the French week and the Friday etymology cut.)

Facts (checked)
  • No astronomical basis; two traditions: "The week as known today is the result of the merging of two different cultural traditions" (Jewish and planetary), converging over the first two centuries CE; "the seven-day cycle itself has no astronomical basis" (Wikipedia, Week; UCL papers by Stern and Bultrighini, who argue the planetary week is a Roman product). The popular "the Babylonians invented the week" is unsettled: an older encyclopedia says even the Babylonians had no seven-day week. So the script does not credit Babylon.
  • The Moon: the synodic month is about 29.53 days.
  • Planetary hours (Cassius Dio, early 3rd century, the earliest source; Vettius Valens similar; Wikipedia, Planetary hours): planets in the "Chaldean order" (Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, Sun, Venus, Mercury, Moon, by apparent speed) rule the hours in turn; the ruler of a day's first hour names the day; 24 = 3 x 7 + 3, so each day moves three places on: Saturn -> Sun -> Moon -> Mars -> Mercury -> Jupiter -> Venus -> Saturn. Checked by hand: it gives the week in order.
  • Armenian day names: like Syriac and Greek (δευτέρα σαββάτων, "second of the week"), Armenian counts the days from the Sabbath week: երկուշաբթի ... հինգշաբթի = day 2 ... 5; շաբաթ via Syriac shabta; կիրակի from Greek Kyriake, "the Lord's day"; ուրբաթ from Syriac ʿrubtā (Greek Paraskeue "preparation" translates it). "«նախօրյակ» իմաստով" (eve) for ʿrubtā is from general knowledge: check before voicing (U-ss).
  • Soviet week: the nepreryvka started in 1929 as a five-day cycle, became six days less than two years later, with staggered days off; a decree of 26 June 1940 restored the seven-day week (History.com; Wikipedia, Soviet calendar). Spouses with different days off: History.com.
  • French ten-day week: the Republican calendar (from 1793) used the ten-day décade; Napoleon abolished the calendar on 1 January 1806 (World History Encyclopedia).
  • Constantine's Sunday (321) is left out: one idea per video.
