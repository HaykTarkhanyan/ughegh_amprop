# Short 5: Why waiting feels longer than it is, and how airports and Disney hide wait times - script and plan

Draft for Hayk's review, 2026-11-25. No voice until the script is approved. Script source: `beats.hy.json`.

## Choices (carried over, not asked again)

Real examples, dots where counting helps (people in queues), ~2.5 min, one brief card (Maister's rules as pairs).

## The idea in one paragraph

Standing still makes time stretch. The famous Houston airport story (make people walk longer to the baggage carousel
and complaints vanish) is unverified, but the idea behind it is well studied: Maister's 1985 rules say occupied time
feels shorter than empty time, and unknown, unexplained, unfair and solo waits feel longer. Disney posts waits that
are longer than the real ones (fans measure about two thirds) and folds its queues so you never see their length,
often with games along the way. Progress bars, taxi maps and pizza trackers remove the uncertainty. And the other
line is not faster: with three equal lines, yours wins only one time in three, and the two losses are the ones you
remember. That is why a single snake line is fairer and, on average, faster.

**3. honest.** Այս պատմությունն ամենուր կրկնում են, բայց ոչ ոք չի կարողանում հաստատել, թե որ օդանավակայանն էր և երբ։ Սակայն դրա հետևում եղած գաղափարը լավ ուսումնասիրված է։

**4. empty.** 1985 թվականին Դեյվիդ Մայստերը գրեց սպասման հոգեբանության մասին։ Նրա առաջին կանոնը՝ զբաղված ժամանակն ավելի կարճ է թվում, քան դատարկը։ Երբ ոչինչ չես անում, ուշադրությունդ ժամացույցի վրա է, և ամեն րոպե ձգվում է։ Հետազոտություններում մարդիկ սովորաբար գերագնահատում են, թե որքան են սպասել։

## The numbers and facts (checked)

- **Houston baggage story** (beats 2-3): widely repeated (walk ~1 min + wait ~7 min; gates moved, walk ~6x longer;
  complaints "near zero"; popularised by Alex Stone, NYT 2012), but no airport, year, study or data has been
  confirmed; one article calls it unverifiable. Told as «Պատմում են» plus an explicit "unverified" beat.
- **Maister 1985** (beats 4-5): "The Psychology of Waiting Lines", eight propositions, confirmed on his own blog
  (occupied < unoccupied, pre-process > in-process, anxiety, uncertain > known, unexplained > explained, unfair >
  equitable, valuable service -> longer tolerated, solo > group). The script uses five.
- **"People overestimate their waits"** (beat 4): Hornik, *J. Consumer Research* 11 (1984); the often quoted "36%"
  appears only in a secondary source and is ambiguous, so the script gives no number.
- **Disney posted vs actual** (beat 6): TouringPlans (user-submitted timings, a company that sells predictions):
  actual waits ~60% of posted at EPCOT, ~64-68% at the other parks; at Animal Kingdom actual < posted in ~75% of
  45,593 pairs (2019). Disney gives no official reason (TouringPlans suggests crowd control), so the script claims
  none.
- **Disney queues** (beat 7): themed and interactive queues confirmed (Space Mountain games; Haunted Mansion extended
  interactive queue, 2013; some physical games later moved to an app). That Disney re-routes chains to change how
  long a line looks is one line in a TouringPlans post; the script only says the queue folds out of sight.
- **The three lines** (beat 9): `python waiting/simulate_lines.py` (seed 509): 3 equal lines, 4 people ahead, mean
  1.5 min each: your line was fastest in 10 of 30 trips; over 100,000 trips 0.335 (one in three, by symmetry).
- **One snake line** (beat 10): standard queueing result: one shared line feeding several counters has a lower average
  wait than separate lines (pooling), and no one can overtake you.
