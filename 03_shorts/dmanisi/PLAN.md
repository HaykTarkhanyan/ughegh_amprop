# Short: The toothless man from Dmanisi (the oldest evidence of compassion?) - script and plan

Draft for Hayk's review, 2026-11-25. Script first (Q17). Rendered in Remotion (DECISIONS.md #21). Script source:
`beats.hy.json`. Prompted by exurb1a's "How to Be Correct About Everything All the Time" (2020, from 11:41:
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DJiGuFCzaFo&t=701s), which tells the jawbone as proof that "someone was feeding him".

## The idea in one paragraph

Almost two million years ago, a man in what is now Georgia, near Armenia, lost all but one tooth and lived several
years more; we know because the empty tooth sockets had filled with bone. The moving story says someone must have
fed him: the oldest evidence of compassion. But the bone only says he lived without teeth, not how; the discoverers
themselves raised the possibility that he fed himself, and a wild bonobo was later seen living long without teeth.
The famous "a healed femur is the first sign of civilisation" quote, credited to Margaret Mead, does not check out
either. Compassion is not made up (Neanderthals healed from terrible injuries), but one bone cannot tell us which
story is true. The most beautiful stories are the ones most worth checking. Same move as the Wald and Houston beats
in the other shorts, and it follows Fable's notes: tension in the first line, no «Այնպես որ,» ending, no summary
card, few spoken years.

## Facts (checked)

- **The fossil** (Lordkipanidze et al., "The earliest toothless hominin skull", *Nature* 434: 717-718, 2005; a 2006
  *Anatomical Record* follow-up): cranium D3444 and jaw D3900, found 2002-2003 at Dmanisi, Georgia; an older adult male
  who "had lost all but one tooth several years before death" (resorbed sockets); the earliest severe chewing
  impairment in the hominin record; about 1.77 million years old ("almost two million" in the script). Dmanisi
  hominins are among the earliest humans known outside Africa.
- **Care, as the discoverers put it:** they suggested others may have helped him and raised "questions about
  alternative subsistence strategies in early Homo". The Smithsonian's Human Origins page is firmer ("members of his
  social group must have taken care of him"). A *Sapiens* essay argues he did not necessarily need help.
- **The bonobo** (Surbeck et al., 2020, Kokolopori, a wild bonobo site): a toothless bonobo ("Sting") survived a long
  time without teeth, used to argue the help-only reading needs reconsidering. Only the summary was read.
- **Margaret Mead's femur quote:** traced only to Paul Brand's secondhand account of a lecture; Snopes calls it
  unanswered, Quote Investigator and *Sapiens* doubt it; in a 1968 interview Mead defined civilisation by cities,
  division of labour and writing.
- **Neanderthal healed injuries** (Spikins et al., "Calculated or caring?", *World Archaeology* 2018, and York press
  release): widespread evidence of recovery from serious injuries; whether it shows managed care or toughness is
  debated, as the script says.
- **The exurb1a passage** (11:41-13:10, from its subtitles): says "male of about 40", "probably 2 million years ago",
  "which means someone was helping him to eat". The script does not quote or name the video.
