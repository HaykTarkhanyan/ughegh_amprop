## The idea in one paragraph

About half of people yawn when they see someone yawn, and even reading about yawning works, so the viewer will
probably yawn during the video. Why we yawn at all is unsettled: the oxygen idea was tested in the 1980s and failed;
brain cooling is a newer theory with critics. Why it is contagious is also debated: yawns pass most from family, then
friends, then acquaintances, then strangers (2011), which fits empathy, but a 328-person study (2014) found empathy
did not predict it (only age, a little), and a 2018 study found the link again. Dogs catch human yawns too, more from
their owners. The honest answer: we do not fully know, and that is the point of the video.

## Facts (checked)

- **About half** (Provine's yawning experiments: 55% in NPR's account; his standard protocol played yawn videos and
  counted yawns; reading about yawning also triggers them, ABC News 2000, NPR 2007).
- **Oxygen idea falsified** (Massen & Gallup 2017 review: "tested and subsequently falsified 30 years ago"); the script
  says "already in the 1980s".
- **Brain cooling** (Gallup & Gallup, *Evolutionary Psychology* 2007): yawning "serves to keep the brain in thermal
  homeostasis"; defenders say no study contradicts it, critics disagree; the script says it has doubters.
- **Kin > friends > acquaintances > strangers** (Norscia & Palagi, *PLoS ONE* 2011): 109 adults observed for a year in
  natural settings in several countries; contagion and speed of response highest for kin. A critic (Gallup) noted we
  may notice yawns more in people close to us.
- **Empathy not predictive** (Bartholomew & Cirulli, *PLoS ONE* 9(3), 2014, Duke): 328 participants, a 3-minute yawn
  video; 222 yawned at least once; after age, empathy, time of day and intelligence had no effect; age explained 8%.
  A 2018 *J. Exp. Psychology: General* study (samples of 171 and 333) found contagious yawning related to empathy.
- **Dogs** (Joly-Mascheroni, Senju & Shepherd, *Biology Letters* 4(5) 2008): 21 of 29 dogs (72%) yawned at a human's
  yawn, none in the control; a later study found dogs respond more to their owner's yawns than a stranger's.
- **Left out:** the seasonal-climate study (not confirmed), children's development and autism claims (not checked),
  "mirror neurons" (often invoked, evidence mixed).
