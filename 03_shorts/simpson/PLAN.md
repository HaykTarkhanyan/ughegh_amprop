# Short 3: Simpson's paradox ("better in every group, worse overall") - script and plan

Draft for Hayk's review, 2026-11-25. No voice until the script is approved. Script source: `beats.hy.json`.

## Choices (carried over from the Bayes short, not asked again)

Hayk asked for this one without new instructions, so the Bayes answers are reused: real examples, people shown as
dots (counting, not probabilities), a deeper ~2.5 min video, one brief formula-style card (here: "the overall rate is
a weighted average"). Change any of these in the review.

## The idea in one paragraph

Two treatments for kidney stones: A cures 78%, B cures 83%, so B looks better. But A is better for small stones AND
for large stones. The overall number lies because A got most of the hard cases (large stones) and B most of the easy
ones: the overall rate is a weighted average, and the weights differ. The same thing made Berkeley look biased
against women in 1973 (women applied to the hard departments) and made Italy's COVID death rate look worse than
China's in early 2020 (Italy's cases were older). Rule: when the groups differ in something that existed before the
treatment, compare like with like; when the split is itself a result of the treatment, trust the overall number.

**4. crowd.** Պատկերացրու 350 մարդ ամեն կողմում։ Բաց վիրահատությունից հետո քարից ազատվել է 273-ը, երկրորդ մեթոդից հետո՝ 289-ը։ Երկրորդն ավելի լավն է թվում։

## The numbers (checked)

**Kidney stones** (Charig et al., BMJ 1986; reanalysed as Simpson's paradox by Julious & Mullee, BMJ 1994):

| | A, open surgery | B, through the skin (PCNL) |
|---|---|---|
| all | 273 / 350 = 78.0% | 289 / 350 = 82.6% (said 83) |
| small stones (< 2 cm) | 81 / 87 = 93.1% | 234 / 270 = 86.7% |
| large stones (>= 2 cm) | 192 / 263 = 73.0% | 55 / 80 = 68.8% (said 69) |
| share of hard cases | 263 / 350 = 75% large | 270 / 350 = 77% small |

Sums check: 81 + 192 = 273, 234 + 55 = 289; 263 of the 343 large-stone patients got A, 270 of the 357 small-stone
patients got B.

**Berkeley, fall 1973** (Bickel, Hammel & O'Connell, Science 187, 1975): campus-wide 44% of 8,442 men and 35% of
4,321 women admitted (spoken). Six largest departments (applicants, admission rate):

| Dept | Men | Women |
|---|---|---|
| A | 825, 62% | 108, 82% |
| B | 560, 63% | 25, 68% |
| C | 325, 37% | 593, 34% |
| D | 417, 33% | 375, 35% |
| E | 191, 28% | 393, 24% |
| F | 373, 6% | 341, 7% |
| all six | 2,691, 44.5% | 1,835, 30.4% |

Women did better in 4 of 6 (A, B, D, F). For the picture, A+B are grouped as "easy" (men 62.5%, women 79.7%) and C-F
as "hard" (men 25.5%, women 26.5%); 92.8% of women but 48.5% of men applied to the hard ones. Verified 2026-11-25
against R's built-in `UCBAdmissions` dataset (raw admitted / rejected counts from Bickel et al., via the Rdatasets
CSV): every department figure matches; the paper's own PDF could not be fetched (404 / 403).

**COVID-19, early 2020** (von Kügelgen, Gresele & Schölkopf, IEEE Trans. AI 2021; arXiv 2005.07180): China (data of
17 Feb 2020) vs Italy (9 Mar 2020): Italy's case fatality rate lower in every age group, higher in total, because a
larger share of Italy's confirmed cases were elderly. China's total 2.3% (1,023 / 44,672). Italy's exact figures
were not read, so the script gives no numbers and the bars are schematic. To read the paper's Table before
rendering if exact bars are wanted.

## Caveats kept out of the script

- The kidney-stone comparison was not randomised: open surgery was done in 1972-80, PCNL in 1980-85. Stone size is
  the confounder the paradox is about, but the periods differ too.
- Berkeley: the paper found a small bias in favour of women once departments are accounted for, and pointed at
  earlier stages of education for the different choices. Berkeley was afraid of a lawsuit but was not sued (Bickel
  interview), so the script does not say it was.
- Beat 12's rule is the standard causal-inference advice (split by causes that come before the treatment, not by its
  results), simplified to one sentence.

- **Split:** the dots move (not fade) into a small-stone band and a large-stone band per crowd, so it is visible that
  A's crowd is mostly large stones and B's mostly small.
- **Weighted card:** each treatment's rate drawn as two bar segments whose widths are the group sizes.
- **Berkeley:** 100 men vs 100 women, each split easy / hard (51 / 49 and 7 / 93), admitted ones lit.
- **Reused:** the crowd-grid component planned for the Bayes short (this short needs the same grid with a "move into
  bands" state), captions, karaoke ending, palette, font.
