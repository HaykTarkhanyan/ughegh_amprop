The idea

Every secure connection starts with two strangers agreeing on a key while anyone can listen. The video explains how, twice: first with paint (the intuition), then with real numbers on a clock (the actual mechanism, small enough to follow). Then three honest limits: the method was found earlier in secret, it does not stop someone pretending to be the other side (hence certificates), and a large quantum computer would break it (hence the new exchanges browsers already add). The closing line is the one idea: mixing is easy, unmixing is practically impossible.

Structure
  1. Hook and problem (beats 1-3): your phone just agreed a secret key in public; couriers and code books worked for centuries; the internet has no couriers.
  2. Paint (beats 4-10): Ani, Aram, Eva; common yellow, secret red and blue, orange and green cross the line, both reach the same brown, Eva cannot unmix. Same story as the Short, with room to breathe.
  3. Clock arithmetic (beats 11-13): the paint is only an analogy; on a clock 10 + 5 = 3; powers on a 23-hour clock jump around with no visible order (5, 2, 10, 4, 20, 8).
  4. The real exchange (beats 14-15, the showcase): 5 and 23 public, secrets 6 and 15, 8 and 19 sent, both get 2; Eva would need the 6, easy for small numbers, practically impossible for properly chosen huge ones.
  5. Three limits (beats 16-19): GCHQ had it first and kept it secret until 1997; Eva in the middle (why sites have certificates); quantum computers (why browsers already add a new exchange).
  6. Close (beat 20): «Խառնելը՝ հեշտ, բաժանելը՝ գործնականում անհնար» (U4).

18. certificates. Դրա համար փոխանակումից բացի պետք է համոզվել, թե ում հետ ես խոսում։ Կայքերն ունեն վկայականներ՝ թվային ստորագրություններ, որոնք հաստատում են վստահելի կազմակերպությունները, և քո բրաուզերը ստուգում է դրանք ամեն անգամ։

Facts (checked 2026-11-26)
  • Diffie & Hellman 1976, "New Directions in Cryptography", IEEE Trans. Information Theory 22(6): 644-654, https://doi.org/10.1109/TIT.1976.1055638 (checked in OpenAlex). Named on the source line only; Merkle's related work could join the plan's notes if Hayk wants the "Diffie-Hellman-Merkle" credit.
  • GCHQ first: James Ellis, "non-secret encryption" (1970; some sources 1969); Clifford Cocks, essentially RSA (1973); Malcolm Williamson, a Diffie-Hellman-like exchange (1974); made public in December 1997 (Cocks's talk on 18 Dec 1997); Ellis died on 25 Nov 1997. From the crypto_paint plan: GCHQ's Williamson biography, Schneier's "The Secret Story of Nonsecret Encryption" (1998), the NSA / defense.gov Diffie profile.
  • Toy numbers (computed): powers of 5 on a 23-hour clock: 5, 2, 10, 4, 20, 8; 5⁶ mod 23 = 8, 5¹⁵ mod 23 = 19, 19⁶ mod 23 = 2 = 8¹⁵ mod 23.
  • Clock arithmetic: 10 + 5 on a 12-hour clock = 3 (15 = 12 + 3). Real exchanges use a large prime and, more often today, elliptic curves; the voice says "a large prime number", true of classic Diffie-Hellman, and the visual stays with numbers (elliptic curves are not mentioned: one mechanism is enough).
  • "Practically impossible": for properly chosen large parameters there is no known practical fast classical method (Codex X44; RFC 9325, https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc9325/, specifies minimum sizes); the voice says "with ordinary computers" and the next beat explains the quantum exception. Wording «գործնականում անհնար»: Hayk, U4.
  • Man in the middle and certificates: standard; TLS authenticates the server with a certificate chain to a trusted authority.
  • Quantum: Shor's algorithm (1994) solves the underlying problem efficiently on a large enough quantum computer. NIST published ML-KEM (derived from Kyber) as FIPS 203 on 13 August 2024 (https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/fips/203/final). Chrome moved from a pre-standard Kyber hybrid to the hybrid X25519MLKEM768 in Chrome 131, late 2024 (https://security.googleblog.com/2024/09/a-new-path-for-kyber-on-web.html); a vendor guide says Edge and Firefox also negotiate it by default. The voice says «շատ բրաուզերներ ... հին ու նոր ... միասին» (hybrid). Re-check the current browser defaults before publishing: nothing official for 2026 was found.
