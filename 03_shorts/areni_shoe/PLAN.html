The idea in one paragraph

A roughly 5,500-year-old leather shoe from Areni-1 survives whole, including its lacing. The showcase folds a schematic single leather piece around a right-foot form, then returns to the real photograph. Its 24.5 cm length and approximate European size 37 give scale, not the wearer's sex. Dating the leather gives a range rather than an exact birthday. After this solid evidence, the doubt beat limits the record to surviving leather footwear: older plant-fibre sandals exist, and finding a shoe does not locate the invention of footwear. The single extension is preservation: cave conditions, the covering deposit, and the grass whose presence is observed but purpose inferred. The close returns to the object in the History Museum of Armenia collection and its unknown owner.

Facts (checked)

Evidence base: _knowledge/short_videos/facts_codex_5_mixed_2026-11-28.md, K2. Each source below is in the sheet. The analytical paper and the record listing were also reopened during drafting. No quotation is invented.

  • Find location and discovery year - high (find): discovered in 2008 at Areni-1, Vayots Dzor, Armenia. The year is on the source line, not in the voice. History Museum of Armenia, official catalogue.
  • Age and calibrated interval - high (hook, dating): approximately 5,500 years old; combined leather result 3627-3377 calibrated BCE, from Table 2. The screen uses this range, not the inconsistent narrow interval elsewhere in the paper or the grass result. Samples are from the leather itself; no raw BP value is presented as calendar years ago. Pinhasi et al., 2010, Table 2.
  • Construction, lacing and right foot - high (hook, find, construction): a single piece of hide wrapped around the foot; leather lacing survives. Wear and shape identify the right foot. The fold animation is an explanatory reconstruction, not a recovered cutting pattern. Pinhasi et al., Results, UCLA's research announcement, 2010.
  • Dimensions - high (construction): length 24.5 cm, approximately European size 37. Modern size comparison does not establish the wearer's sex. Pinhasi et al., Results, UCLA. No US size conversion is used.
  • Record category - high (find, doubt): oldest known surviving leather shoe, not oldest footwear of every material. Older plant-fibre sandals survive. The discovery provides no evidence of an invention site or the wearer's ethnicity. Pinhasi et al., Discussion, Guinness, First leather shoe.
  • Preservation - high for observed conditions; medium for causal contributions (preservation): the researchers describe stable, cool, dry conditions and a thick sheep-dung covering. Their explanation links the combined conditions to preservation. The script attributes the explanation and gives no measured share to any factor. UCLA. No temperature, humidity percentage or chemical tanning mechanism is claimed.
  • Grass - high for presence; medium for purpose (grass): grass was found inside. Insulation and keeping the shoe's shape/storage are hypotheses, with the paper favouring the latter. Both are voiced as possibilities, followed by the explicit fact/inference distinction. Pinhasi et al., Results, UCLA.
  • Museum collection and unknown owner - high (closing): the official museum catalogue records the object in the History Museum of Armenia collection. The paper cannot determine the wearer's sex; no individual is identified. The script avoids asserting that a particular gallery display is open today. Museum catalogue, Pinhasi et al..
  • Medium-or-lower claims used: only the preservation explanation and the possible grass functions above. No low-confidence claim is used. Cowhide identification is omitted; the material is called leather.
Animation plan (showcase)
  • One showcase, construction: frame 0 contains the real shoe photo, a schematic leather outline and the persistent «վերակազմություն» tag. Fold the outline around a neutral right-foot form and thread the leather lacing. Keep a 24.5 cm ruler and approximate European size 37 for scale. The viewer follows the fold through the 4 s pause. This explains one-piece construction; it is not an exact ancient manufacturing sequence. Do not invent hole counts, tools, hide species, a separate rigid sole, or the wearer's appearance.
  • Other beats: hook starts on the real lacing; find pairs the cave entrance and shoe photographs. dating shows only the verified combined leather interval, 3627-3377 BCE. doubt underlines the leather qualifier and uses a generic fibre-sandal icon labelled «նկարազարդում», not an attributed archaeological specimen.
  • Preservation extension: an illustrative cave cross-section shows the covering deposit and cool/dry icons, without quantitative environmental claims. The grass cutaway retains «նկարազարդում» and marks both proposed uses as hypotheses. The 2 s pauses let the viewer read the conditions and distinguish presence from purpose.
  • Closing: return to the real shoe photo with the museum-collection caption; leave the owner field unanswered. The voice says the closing screen line. No summary card or fictional display case. Total marked pauses: 10 s; longest: 4 s on the showcase. Every beat starts with a visible object from its narration.
