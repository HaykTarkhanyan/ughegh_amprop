Draft for Hayk's review, 2026-11-25. Script first (Q17), rendered in Remotion (DECISIONS.md #21). Script source:

## The idea in one paragraph

The medium popcorn sells the large, even when nobody buys the medium. Small 1000 dram, large 2000: which do you
take? Now add a medium at 1800, and the large suddenly looks like a gift, only 200 more. That third option, which
almost nobody takes, changes how we compare the other two: the decoy effect. The famous demonstration: a magazine
offered web-only $59, print-only $125, print and web $125; 84% chose the bundle, and without the useless print-only
option only 32% did. It works because comparing is easy and judging a price is hard. But the effect is weaker than
its fame: nineteen studies with real products people could see and taste found none; it holds when the choice is
about numbers, and popcorn prices are numbers. The takeaway: cross out the option nobody takes, then choose.
(After the Fable review: the hook claims the effect, not the cinema's intent, and the viewer chooses before the
medium appears.)

## Facts (checked)

- **Origin:** Huber, Payne & Puto, "Adding asymmetrically dominated alternatives", *Journal of Consumer Research*
  1982 (presented 1981): a decoy raised the target's share for beer, cars, restaurants, films and TV sets, but not
  lottery tickets (Wikipedia, Decoy effect).
- **The magazine demo** (Ariely, *Predictably Irrational*, 2008; The Economist's 2009 piece "The importance of
  irrelevant alternatives"): Economist.com $59, print $125, print & web $125; about 100 MIT students; with the decoy
  16% / 0% / 84%; without it 68% web-only and 32% print & web. One Wikipedia version garbles the split; the 84 / 16
  and 68 / 32 figures are the book's and the detailed retellings'. It is a classroom demonstration, not a
  peer-reviewed study: the script calls it «ցուցադրում» and does not name Ariely in the voice. **Context:** a 2012
  paper he co-authored was retracted in 2021 over fabricated data (an unrelated study); the source line can carry
  his book, or be left off (U-xx).
- **Limits:** Frederick, Lee & Baskin, "The limits of attraction", *Journal of Marketing Research* 2014: 19 studies
  with "natural" stimuli (attributes people experience directly) found no attraction effect, and decoys were as
  likely to repel. The effect is reliable when attributes are numbers and fails with perceptual stimuli (Kellogg
  working paper). Huber, Payne & Puto answered (2014) that it replicates, with moderators.
- **Popcorn prices** are invented, labelled «օրինակ» on screen; no cinema is named, and the hook says «հաճախ», not
  "always": no source shows a cinema designing its medium as a decoy.
