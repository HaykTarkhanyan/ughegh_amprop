# The Wald bomber story is partly legend, and the red-dot plane is a 2021 illustration

**Context:** the survivorship-bias short uses the classic story: officers wanted to armour returning bombers where
the bullet holes were, and the statistician Abraham Wald said to armour where there were none.

**Checked 2026-11-25 (after a heads-up note):**
- Commons describes File:Survivorship-bias.svg as "Illustration of hypothetical damage pattern on a WW2 bomber. Loosely
  based on data from an unillustrated report by Abraham Wald (1943)", drawn 2021 (Martin Grandjean, McGeddon). It is
  not wartime data.
- Wald's 1943 memos are real, but they are equations for estimating vulnerability from returning planes. Secondary
  sources (a 2024 blog citing Bill Casselman's AMS feature column, Bill Sweetman) find no record of the "officers
  wanted it the other way" scene; others repeat it.

**Consequences:** the short says «Պատմում են, որ ...» ("the story goes that ...") and labels the picture
«նկարազարդում» ("illustration"). For a channel about thinking errors, repeating a too-neat story is the kind of
mistake commenters catch. The same care applies to the fridge claim itself (lit review section 12: partly true).
