# Fact sheets for «Ուղեղ Ամպրոպ»

Checked 28 November 2026. **High** means well supported as worded; **medium** means qualified, model-dependent, or dependent on a historical account. Quotes below are verbatim excerpts or exact numerical entries. **Calculated** values are distinguished from source measurements. Hooks and showcase suggestions reuse the evidence listed under each topic.

## C5. Leap years and the 10 lost days of 1582

**1. Verdict on the premise**

**True with a geographical qualification.** Where the Gregorian reform initially took effect, Thursday 4 October 1582 was followed by Friday 15 October. Ten **date labels** were omitted; ordinary time and the weekday sequence continued.

Accurate wording: “The Julian calendar slowly slipped against the seasons. In 1582, the Gregorian reform skipped ten dates and changed the leap-year rule.”

**2. Key facts**

- **High — Year lengths.** A modern approximate mean tropical year is **365.2422 days**; the Julian calendar averages **365.25 days**. These are different kinds of quantities: an astronomical approximation and a calendar average. **Exact source values:** “365.2422 days”; “365.25 days.” [U.S. Naval Observatory, *Leap Years*](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/faq/leap_years).

- **High — Julian drift.** USNO gives the annual excess as approximately **0.0078 day**. **Calculated:** about **674 seconds**, or **11 minutes 14 seconds per year**; approximately **one day in 128 years**. These calculations use the rounded tropical-year value above. **Quote:** “0.0078 days.” [USNO](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/faq/leap_years).

- **High — Why the correction was ten days.** The bull’s stated reference was the equinox’s displacement **since the Council of Nicaea**, rather than the entire accumulated displacement since Julius Caesar. **Quote:** “about ten days since the Council of Nicaea.” [Gregory XIII, *Inter gravissimas*, 1582, English translation with links to the Clavius facsimile](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Translation:Inter_gravissimas). Say “about ten days of displacement by 1582”; avoid pretending that multiplying a modern rounded annual error reconstructs every historical equinox exactly.

- **High — The date and weekday sequence.** The change was **Thursday 4 October → Friday 15 October 1582**. **Exact excerpts:** “4 October 1582 (Julian) is a Thursday”; “15 October 1582 (Gregorian) is a Friday.” [USNO, *Julian Date Converter*, explanatory notes](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/data/JulianDate).

- **High — The Gregorian century rule.** Years divisible by four are leap years, except century years must also be divisible by **400**. Consequently **1900 was not leap; 2000 was leap**. There are **97 leap years per 400 years**. **Exact source entries:** “1900”; “2000”; “97 leap days”; “400 years.” [USNO](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/faq/leap_years).

- **High — Remaining Gregorian error, approximately.** The calendar averages **365.2425 days**. Against 365.2422, its excess is **calculated as about 26 seconds per year**, equivalent to roughly **one day in 3,300 years**. This is an approximation, not a prediction of an inevitable correction on a particular future date. **Exact source values:** “365.2425 days”; “3300 years.” [USNO](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/faq/leap_years).

- **High — Britain, 1752.** **2 September was followed by 14 September**, omitting **11 dates**. The statute is the strongest source. **Quote:** “omitting for that time only the eleven intermediate nominal days.” [*Calendar (New Style) Act 1750*, section 1](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/apgb/Geo2/24/23/section/1).

- **High — Soviet Russia, 1918.** **31 January was followed by 14 February**, omitting **13 dates** in civil reckoning. **Exact decree excerpts:** “The first day after January 31, this year”; “not as February 1, but as February 14.” [Council of People’s Commissars, decree reproduced in *First Decrees of Soviet Power*, p. 93](https://www.marxists.org/history/ussr/government/1917/firstdecreesofsovietpower.pdf). A contemporary diplomatic telegram independently reports “February 1 which decree designates February 14.” [U.S. State Department historical documents, 1918](https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1918Russiav01/d366).

- **High for the year — Armenian Church adoption.** An official Armenian diocesan explanation supports **1923**. **Quote:** “since 1923, the Armenian Church has been using the new Gregorian calendar.” [Armenian Church, Diocese of the United Kingdom and Ireland](https://www.armenianchurch.org.uk/our-faith). This supports the year, without independently establishing the precise decree date or implementation sequence.

- **High — Jerusalem exception.** The Armenian Apostolic Church’s Western European legation explicitly identifies Jerusalem as retaining the Julian calendar. **Quote:** “the Jerusalem See follows the Jullian calendar.” [Official church calendar explanation](https://www.armenian-church.eu/en/holidays/). The spelling is the source’s.

- **High — Armenian Christmas dates.** At Etchmiadzin, the combined Nativity and Theophany feast is celebrated on **6 January**. **Exact excerpts:** “On January 6”; “Feast of the Holy Nativity and Theophany.” [Mother See, 6 January 2026](https://www.armenianchurch.org/en/news/patriarchal-divine-liturgy-on-the-occasion-of-the-feast-of-the-holy-nativity-and-theophany/12110). In Jerusalem’s present calendar relationship, **6 January Julian corresponds to 19 January Gregorian**, with the eve on 18 January. **Quote:** “Christmas Eve and Christmas Day are celebrated on January 18-19.” [Eastern Diocese’s 2026 Holy Land itinerary](https://armenianchurch.us/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/HL26_011526X_43749_Armenian-Church_Chevian_005-1.pdf).

- **High — The famous British calendar riots are a myth.** Historian Robert Poole’s archival investigation concludes: **“It can be asserted with confidence that the calendar riots are a myth.”** [Poole, 1995, *Past & Present*, p. 104](https://insight.cumbria.ac.uk/684/1/Poole_GiveUsOurEleven.pdf). There was controversy and resistance; that does not establish the familiar story of mobs believing their lives had been shortened. **Conflicting retelling:** an NPS report says “leading to riots.” [NPS, 2023, p. 305](https://www.nps.gov/gewa/learn/historyculture/upload/GEWA-HRS-2023-508-2.pdf). **Trust Poole:** his article directly investigates the evidence; the NPS passage is a brief derivative account that actually cites him.

**3. Retellings to avoid**

- “The whole world lost ten days in 1582.” Adoption happened at different times.
- “People lived ten fewer days.” Date labels changed.
- “Every fourth year is a leap year.” That describes the basic Julian rule and omits Gregorian exceptions.
- “The Gregorian calendar is perfectly accurate.”
- Presenting Hogarth’s election satire or “Give us our eleven days!” as documentary proof of calendar riots.
- Calling **1923** Armenia’s civil-calendar adoption date on the strength of a source about the **Church**.
- Explaining Armenian Apostolic Christmas on 6 January merely as a calendar conversion of 25 December. The cited church sources identify the combined Nativity–Theophany feast.

**4. Best hook**

“Ten dates vanished overnight. In places adopting the reform in 1582, Thursday October 4 was followed by Friday October 15.”

**5. Showcase idea**

Animate October’s calendar: **4 → 15**, while a continuous clock and **Thursday → Friday** remain visible. Then show the repair: **1900: no leap day; 2000: leap day; 97 leap days in 400 years**.

Use the Armenian Christmas calendar comparison as the single extension.

*Small source line:* “Gregory XIII, 1582 • USNO • Robert Poole, 1995 • Armenian Church calendar sources.”

**6. Images**

**High; checked on Commons:** [**File:Inter-grav.jpg**](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Inter-grav.jpg) — first page of the bull; **public domain**. The licensing panel states “Public domain” and identifies the work as published before the applicable U.S. cutoff.

**7. Armenian anchor**

**Solid:** the Church’s **1923** adoption, Jerusalem’s Julian exception, and the present **6 January / 19 January** relationship, documented above. Specify **Armenian Apostolic** practice.

**8. Open questions**

- The precise 1923 decree date and implementation details need the original ecclesiastical document before appearing onscreen.
- The tropical year changes slowly; **26 seconds/year** and **3,300 years/day** are approximate comparisons.
- The Julian–Gregorian difference is not permanently thirteen days. Avoid wording the Christmas conversion as timeless.

## C6. Why the sky is blue and sunsets are red

**1. Verdict on the premise**

**True for the usual clear daytime sky and reddening toward sunset.** Air molecules scatter shorter visible wavelengths more strongly. A low Sun’s direct light crosses more atmosphere, losing a greater proportion of its blue light from the direct beam.

Accurate wording: “The same wavelength-dependent scattering helps make the daytime sky blue and the setting Sun redder.”

**2. Key facts**

- **High — Sunlight contains a spectrum.** It is not a single yellow wavelength. **Quote:** “made up of all the colors of the rainbow.” [NASA Space Place, *Why Is the Sky Blue?*](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/blue-sky/en/).

- **High — Air molecules are sufficient to produce the blue daytime sky.** Dust is not required. **Exact excerpt:** “tiny molecules of air.” [NASA Space Place](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/blue-sky/en/). Smith’s quantitative treatment identifies scattering primarily by oxygen and nitrogen. **Quote:** “mainly oxygen and nitrogen molecules.” [Glenn S. Smith, 2005, p. 590](https://www.physics.smu.edu/scalise/P3374fa19/notes/skyblue/blueSkyHumanResponse.pdf).

- **High — Rayleigh’s wavelength law is approximate.** For scatterers much smaller than the wavelength, scattering varies approximately as **λ⁻⁴**. **Exact expression:** “Qₛ ∝ λ⁻⁴.” [Lakkala et al., 2016, *Visualizing Rayleigh Scattering through UV Photography*](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/bams/97/9/bams-d-14-00260.1.xml). Real air’s refractive properties introduce a small additional wavelength dependence; “approximately” matters.

- **High — The requested blue/red ratio.** **Calculated from that approximation:**  
  \[
  \frac{\sigma(450\,\mathrm{nm})}{\sigma(700\,\mathrm{nm})}
  \approx\left(\frac{700}{450}\right)^4=5.855\approx5.9.
  \]
  This compares scattering strengths for equal incident intensities, not the perceived brightness of blue and red sky. **Supporting source expression:** “λ⁻⁴.” [Lakkala et al., 2016](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/bams/97/9/bams-d-14-00260.1.xml).

- **High — Tyndall and Rayleigh need separate dates.** Tyndall demonstrated blue side-scattering and reddened transmitted light experimentally; the Royal Institution dates its apparatus to **1869**. **Exact entry:** “Date: 1869.” [Royal Institution collection](https://www.rigb.org/explore-science/explore/collection/john-tyndalls-blue-sky-apparatus). Strutt’s theoretical work was published in **1871**; the molecular explanation was established later. **Exact excerpts:** “In 1871 John William Strutt”; “Only in 1899.” [Lange, Rozanov and von Savigny, 2023, historical discussion and original-paper references](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/23/14829/2023/).

- **High — Why blue rather than violet requires vision as well as scattering.** The incoming solar spectrum is not flat, and the eye combines responses across the spectrum. **Quote:** “the spectrum of light emission from the Sun is not constant at all wavelengths.” [Philip Gibbs, 1997, university-hosted physics explanation](https://math.ucr.edu/home/baez/physics/General/BlueSky/blue_sky.html). **Quote:** “the human eye is less sensitive to violet than to blue.” [Hong Kong Observatory](https://www.weather.gov.hk/en/education/earth-science/optical-phenomena/00364-why-is-the-sky-blue.html). Smith’s quantitative result describes the perceived match as **“a mixture of a spectral blue light and white light.”** [Smith, 2005](https://www.physics.smu.edu/scalise/P3374fa19/notes/skyblue/blueSkyHumanResponse.pdf).

- **High — Sunset reddening involves the longer atmospheric path.** **Exact excerpt:** “passing through more of the atmosphere.” [NASA Space Place](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/blue-sky/en/). Scattering removes more short-wavelength light from the direct beam. This does not mean every blue photon disappears or that every sunset must look deep red.

- **High — Horizon air mass is about 38, under stated assumptions.** Kasten and Young’s **1989**, standard-atmosphere table gives these rows, with columns **elevation; absolute optical air mass; relative optical air mass; approximation error**:  
  **“0.0 | 394428 | 38.0868 | 0.432”**  
  **“90.0 | 10356 | 1.0000 | 0.029”**  
  Thus **about 38 versus 1** is defensible for their sea-level standard-atmosphere calculation. [Kasten and Young, Table II](https://doi.org/10.1364/AO.28.004735). It is an integrated optical air mass, not a universal geometrical distance multiplier.

- **High — Mars often reverses the familiar colour pattern.** NASA describes the daytime sky as **“an orange or reddish color”** and the sunset region as a **“blue-gray tone.”** [NASA Space Place](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/blue-sky/en/). “Butterscotch” is a descriptive colour term, not a calibrated constant. NASA’s fuller explanation says **“The rest of the sky is yellow to orange”** and attributes the blue near the Sun to fine dust. [NASA, *What Do Sunrises and Sunsets Look Like on Mars?*](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/planets/mars/what-does-a-sunrise-sunset-look-like-on-mars/). Prefer those direct descriptions over saying the entire Martian sky turns blue.

- **High — Rayleigh scattering is not the complete explanation of every sky colour.** Ozone absorption can contribute substantially to the blue zenith sky around sunset. **Exact source terms:** “ozone absorption”; “zenith sky at sunset.” [Lange et al., 2023](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/23/14829/2023/). Keep the principal explanation scoped to clear daytime conditions and sunset reddening.

**3. Retellings to avoid**

- “The sky reflects the ocean.”
- “Blue scatters the most.” Violet scatters more strongly under the same approximation.
- “Violet is completely absorbed before reaching us.”
- “Red light does not scatter.”
- Giving **four times**, **six times**, or **ten times** without specifying wavelengths.
- Calling the horizon path “exactly 38 times longer” everywhere.
- Saying Mars’s entire sunset sky becomes blue, or applying Earth’s molecular explanation unchanged to Martian dust.

**4. Best hook**

“Blue sky. Red sunset. The same air helps create both.”

**5. Showcase idea**

Send equal-intensity **450 nm** and **700 nm** beams through a schematic atmosphere. Display normalized scattering bars **5.86 : 1**. Then lower the Sun and reveal the longer atmospheric path, labelled **“about 38 air masses at the horizon; standard sea-level atmosphere.”**

Finish with Mars: dusty orange daytime sky and a blue region near the setting Sun.

*Small source line:* “Tyndall, 1869 • Strutt/Rayleigh, 1871 • Kasten & Young, 1989 • Smith, 2005 • NASA.”

**6. Images**

**High; checked on Commons:** [**File:Sunset on Mars (50695877607).png**](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sunset_on_Mars_%2850695877607%29.png) — Spirit’s Gusev sunset; Commons lists **CC0 1.0**, with a reviewed “cc-zero” licence. Preserve the mission credit supplied with the original image.

**7. Armenian anchor**

**None found** meeting the requested standard for a specifically documented **Ararat-from-Yerevan alpenglow** example. The available travel descriptions are insufficient to identify the optical mechanism confidently.

**8. Open questions**

- Actual sunset colours depend on aerosol loading, clouds, viewing direction and atmospheric conditions.
- **5.855** is the ideal λ⁻⁴ calculation, not a measured full-spectrum colour ratio.
- A photograph of a pink mountain alone does not establish whether it shows direct reddened sunlight or a particular twilight phenomenon.

## W3. Seasons come from the tilt, not the distance

**1. Verdict on the premise**

**Correct for Earth’s familiar opposite hemispheric seasons.** Axial tilt changes both sunlight’s angle and daylight duration. Earth–Sun distance also varies, so avoid saying distance has absolutely no physical effect.

Accurate wording: “Earth’s tilt produces the opposite seasons of the two hemispheres.”

**2. Key facts**

- **High — Axial tilt.** NASA’s Earth fact sheet gives **23.44°**, measured relative to the perpendicular to the orbital plane. **Exact table entry:** “Obliquity to orbit (deg) 23.44.” [NASA NSSDC, *Earth Fact Sheet*](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/earthfact.html). **23.5°** is an acceptable rounded educational value.

- **High — Perihelion and aphelion distances.** The same sheet gives approximately **147.095 million km** and **152.100 million km**, respectively. Thus **147.1** and **152.1 million km** are appropriate script values. **Exact rows:** “Perihelion (10⁶ km) 147.095”; “Aphelion (10⁶ km) 152.100.” [NASA NSSDC](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/earthfact.html).

- **High — Perihelion currently falls in early January.** It is not permanently fixed to one calendar date. **Quote:** “Currently, Earth reaches perihelion in early January.” [NASA eclipse glossary](https://science.nasa.gov/eclipses/glossary/). For a dated example, USNO gives **“Perihelion | 2026 January 3 | 17:15”**, in Universal Time. [USNO’s 2026 table](https://aa.usno.navy.mil/calculated/seasons?dst=false&submit=Get+Data&tz=0&tz_label=false&tz_sign=-1&year=2026).

- **High — Two linked heating effects.** Summer brings longer daylight and more direct midday sunlight. Low-angle sunlight distributes a beam’s energy over a larger surface area. **Exact excerpts:** “more hours of daylight”; “spread out over a larger area.” [NASA HEAT, Tanya Anderson, *Why Do We Have Seasons?*, p. 15](https://assets.science.nasa.gov/content/dam/science/hpd/heat/teacher-lessons/Why%20Do%20We%20Have%20Seasons_%20by%20Tanya%20Anderson_0.pdf).

- **High — Noon Sun angles at 40° N.** **Calculated**, neglecting atmospheric refraction: approximately **73.44°** above the horizon at the June solstice and **26.56°** at the December solstice: \(90°-40°\pm23.44°\). The quoted tilt input is **“23.44.”** [NASA NSSDC](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/earthfact.html). These are solar-noon elevations, not the tilt of the ground.

- **High for the stated model — Solstice daylight near Yerevan.** Using latitude **40.00° N**, declination **±23.44°**, and the conventional sunrise/sunset zenith **90.833°**, **calculated daylight is about 15 h 01 min in June and 9 h 19 min in December**. At **40.18° N**, the same calculation gives about **15 h 02 min and 9 h 18 min**. **Exact source number:** “90.833”; NOAA identifies the correction as including refraction and the solar disk’s size. [NOAA solar equations, p. 2](https://gml.noaa.gov/grad/solcalc/solareqns.PDF). These assume an unobstructed horizon.

- **High — Why other daylight figures can differ.** If sunrise and sunset mean the Sun’s **centre crossing a geometrical horizon**, with no refraction or disk correction, the **calculated 40° N** results become approximately **14 h 51 min / 9 h 09 min**. This is a different definition, not contradictory astronomy. **Supporting source distinction:** NOAA specifies the conventional zenith as **“90.833”**, rather than 90°. [NOAA equations](https://gml.noaa.gov/grad/solcalc/solareqns.PDF). Reproduction formula:  
  \[
  D=\frac{2}{15}\arccos\!\left(
  \frac{\sin h_0-\sin\phi\sin\delta}{\cos\phi\cos\delta}
  \right),
  \]
  with the inverse cosine in degrees; use \(h_0=-0.833°\) conventionally or \(0°\) geometrically.

- **High — Southern summer occurs around December because that hemisphere tilts sunward.** **Exact excerpt:** “South Pole tilting toward the Sun (around December).” [NASA Space Place, *What Causes the Seasons?*](https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/seasons/en/). Both hemispheres share essentially the same Earth–Sun orbital distance at any moment, while their seasons are opposite.

- **High — The documentary is real and dates to 1987.** AAAS’s resource description lists **“1987”** and says it shows **“interviews with Harvard graduates and faculty who appear confused about what causes the seasons.”** [AAAS Project 2061, *A Private Universe*](https://www.project2061.org/publications/rsl/online/RESEARCH/VIDEOS/FILM1.HTM). Credit Matthew Schneps and Philip Sadler when identifying the film.

- **Medium — The famous interview count is a reported documentary result, not a population survey.** Hazen and Trefil reported **“only 2 of the 23 students queried could answer the question correctly.”** [Hazen and Trefil, 1991, *Achieving Geological Literacy*, introduction](https://hazen.carnegiescience.edu/sites/hazen.gl.ciw.edu/files/611-GeolLiteracy-1991.pdf). Later academic descriptions call the participants graduates, alumni and faculty. **Trust the cautious conclusion:** the film illustrates misconceptions; it does not establish that a particular percentage of all Harvard graduates—or the public—believe the distance explanation.

**3. Retellings to avoid**

- “Northern summer happens because Earth is closer to the Sun.”
- “The hemisphere tilted toward the Sun is warmer because it is physically much closer.”
- Drawing a strongly elongated Earth orbit without labelling the exaggeration.
- “Distance has no effect whatsoever.”
- Calling **21/23** a representative Harvard or worldwide survey statistic.
- Giving daylight durations without specifying latitude, horizon and sunrise definition.

**4. Best hook**

“Earth is closest to the Sun in January—during Armenia’s winter. Our seasons come from its tilt.”

**5. Showcase idea**

Keep Earth’s axis pointing in approximately the same direction as it moves around the Sun. Zoom into **40° N**: the noon Sun changes from approximately **26.6° to 73.4°**, while a daylight arc changes from about **9 h 19 min to 15 h 01 min**.

Flip the globe to show the southern hemisphere experiencing the opposite season. Label the orbital drawing “schematic.”

*Small source line:* “NASA NSSDC • NOAA solar equations • Schneps & Sadler, 1987.”

**6. Images**

**High; checked on Commons:** [**File:Seasons scheme.svg**](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Seasons_scheme.svg) — **CC BY-SA 3.0**, also offered under GFDL. Exact licence label: “CC BY-SA 3.0.” Follow the file page’s attribution chain, including the original and SVG contributors.

**7. Armenian anchor**

**High — Yerevan is a sound local example.** The UN geographical-names dashboard gives **“Latitude 40.1792831815.”** [UNGEGN, Yerevan city record](https://ungegn.un.org/dashboard/cities/details?id=135). This supports using **40.18° N**, or explicitly rounding to **40° N**, in the calculations above.

**8. Open questions**

- Actual visible sunrise and sunset in a particular Yerevan neighbourhood depend on mountains, buildings and atmospheric conditions.
- The original interview sampling and coding records were not checked here; the documentary count should remain attributed.
- For second-level precision, calculate a specified year and exact observing position. Rounded minute values are sufficient for this video.

## S1. Why the night sky is dark: Olbers’ paradox

**1. Verdict on the premise**

**Sound, with the assumptions made explicit.** The puzzle concerns an ideal universe that is spatially unbounded, static, uniformly populated by luminous stars, and has been shining indefinitely.

Accurate wording: “In an eternal, unchanging universe filled uniformly with stars, why wouldn’t every direction be bright? Our universe has a finite history of starlight.”

**2. Key facts**

- **High — The paradox depends on a model.** Infinite space alone does not imply a bright sky. The usual model also assumes an unchanging stellar population and unlimited time for its light to arrive. **Exact source wording:** “unchanging and infinite in size”; “stars fill the Universe uniformly.” [University of Oregon, Olbers’ paradox notes](https://pages.uoregon.edu/imamura/123cs/lecture-5/olbers.html).

- **High — Distance dimming is counterbalanced by additional sources.** For equal-thickness shells in a uniform population, stellar numbers grow as **r²**, while individual flux falls as **1/r²**. **Exact mathematical entries:** “Flux per star ~ 1 / R²”; “Number of stars ~ R²”; “Total Flux ~ R² / R² ~ 1.” [New Mexico State University astronomy notes](https://astronomy.nmsu.edu/geas/lectures/lecture12/slide02.html). This is an idealized mathematical comparison, not a measured star count.

- **High — Kepler and Halley discussed the problem before Olbers.** **Exact excerpt:** “Kepler, Halley → Jean de Cheseaux → Heinrich Olbers [1823].” [University of Oregon](https://pages.uoregon.edu/imamura/123cs/lecture-5/olbers.html). Do not introduce Olbers as the first person ever to ask why the sky is dark.

- **High — The 1823/1826 discrepancy is bibliographical.** Olbers’s article appeared in the almanac **for 1826**, which was **printed in 1823**. **Exact excerpts:** “Astronomisches Jahrbuch für das Jahr 1826”; “printed in 1823.” [William B. Ashworth Jr., Linda Hall Library, 2019](https://www.lindahall.org/about/news/scientist-of-the-day/heinrich-olbers/). **Trust 1823 as the publication date:** this historical-library account identifies and reproduces the volume, explaining the competing year.

- **High — The dominant resolution is the limited history of luminous sources.** Stars and galaxies have not been emitting indefinitely; finite light speed limits the emitting history available to an observer. **Exact excerpt:** “finite lifetime of the galaxies.” [Wesson, Valle and Stabell, 1987, *The Extragalactic Background Light and a Definitive Resolution of Olbers’s Paradox*, p. 601](https://adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1987ApJ...317..601W). Here “lifetime” concerns the history of galactic light emission, not merely the death of individual stars.

- **High — Expansion contributes, but should not be presented as the sole explanation.** Expansion reduces photon energies through redshift and dilutes radiation. Wesson and colleagues found their modeled background intensity **“only affected by a factor of about 2”** by expansion. [Wesson et al., 1987, abstract](https://adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1987ApJ...317..601W). That factor belongs to their specified model comparisons; it is not a universal exact correction for every wavelength or cosmology.

- **High — Dust cannot permanently hide the energy in the eternal equilibrium version.** Absorbing material heats and re-emits radiation. **Quote:** “the dust will heat up too.” [MIT-hosted explanation](https://web.mit.edu/cdsdev/old/uu-gna/text/astro/universe/olbers.html). Dust affects real observations, but is not a sufficient resolution of the classical paradox.

- **High — Approximately 13.8 billion years is an appropriate universe-age value.** It is an inferred cosmological age, not the time stars have existed everywhere. **Exact source value:** “13.8 billion years old.” [NASA/JPL, Planck results, 2013](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/planck-mission-brings-universe-into-sharp-focus/). Use **“about”**; do not add unjustified decimal places.

- **High — Poe’s 1848 anticipation is real but frequently overstated.** In *Eureka*, he describes a possible background so distant that **“no ray from it has yet been able to reach us at all.”** He immediately qualifies this with **“not even the shadow of a reason”** for believing that possibility. [Poe, *Eureka*, 1848, section VII, pp. 100–101](https://www.eapoe.org/works/essays/eurekae7.htm). Accurate characterization: he articulated an important possibility; this passage is not a confident proof of the modern explanation.

- **High — “Dark” does not mean absolutely radiation-free.** Intergalactic space contains a faint extragalactic background. **Exact excerpt:** “not absolutely black.” [University of California HIPACC, *Measuring Olbers’s Paradox*, 2013](https://hipacc.ucsc.edu/AstroShorts/September2013/MeasuringOlbers_AstroShort_Sept2013.pdf). The video should explain the absence of a star-bright visible sky, not the absence of every photon.

**3. Retellings to avoid**

- “There are simply too few stars,” without explaining the paradox’s assumptions.
- “Distant stars are faint,” while ignoring the increasing number of sources.
- “Dust absorbs all the missing light.”
- “Expansion alone solves it.”
- “Poe proved the Big Bang in 1848.”
- “An age of 13.8 billion years means the observable universe’s present radius is 13.8 billion light-years.” Age and present distance are different quantities in an expanding universe; leave that radius out of this short.

**4. Best hook**

“Why isn’t the whole night sky as bright as a star? Darkness tells us something about the universe’s history.”

**5. Showcase idea**

Build equal-thickness shells around an observer. Move from **r to 2r**: show **four times as many sources**, each contributing **one-quarter** the flux. Their shell contribution stays unchanged.

Then replace the endless, eternal construction with a finite timeline labelled **“Universe: about 13.8 billion years.”** Show that luminous sources begin later. Use Poe’s carefully qualified anticipation as the single extension.

*Small source line:* “Olbers, 1823 • Poe, 1848 • Wesson, Valle & Stabell, 1987 • NASA/Planck, 2013.”

**8. Open questions**

## U2. Why detailed weather forecasts become uncertain beyond roughly a week or two

**1. Verdict on the premise**

**“Forecasts stop at about ten days” is too absolute.** Forecast products extend beyond ten days. What degrades is the ability to predict a particular detailed weather evolution, with the useful horizon depending on the variable, scale, location, situation and skill criterion.

Accurate wording: “Tiny uncertainties grow, limiting detailed day-to-day forecasts. Ensembles help describe the remaining possibilities.”

**2. Key facts**

- **High for the year — Lorenz’s rounding discovery belongs to 1961.** MIT’s historical exhibit describes rounded inputs producing different simulation results and identifies the surviving material as **“Lorenz’s 1961 printouts.”** [MIT, Edward Norton Lorenz exhibit](https://paocweb.mit.edu/files/LorenzPanel.pdf). Avoid asserting a precise January date: the Library of Congress finding aid instead says **“Late in 1961.”** [Library of Congress](https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/19/resources/5517). **Trust the year; leave the month unspecified.**

- **Medium — The famous digits are a historical account.** Peter Dizikes’s 2011 MIT Technology Review account describes rounding **“.506127 to .506.”** [University-hosted copy, p. 2](https://stem.elearning.unipd.it/pluginfile.php/774406/mod_folder/content/0/SistemiComplessi/When%20the%20Butterfly%20Effect%20Took%20Flight%20MIT%20Technology%20Review.pdf?forcedownload=1). **Calculated difference: 0.000127.** Attribute the anecdote; these exact digits were not independently checked here against an original machine record. Do not attach an invented temperature unit to them.

- **High — The scientific result was published in 1963.** Lorenz’s *Deterministic Nonperiodic Flow* demonstrates sensitive dependence in a deterministic model. **Quote:** “slightly differing initial states can evolve into considerably different states.” [Lorenz, 1963, abstract, original paper](https://cdanfort.w3.uvm.edu/research/lorenz-1963.pdf).

- **High — The butterfly talk’s title and date.** Lorenz presented it on **29 December 1972**. **Exact title:** “Predictability: Does the Flap of a Butterfly’s Wings in Brazil Set off a Tornado in Texas?” The date appears as **“December 29, 1972.”** [Lorenz, reproduced in *The Essence of Chaos*, Appendix 1](https://climate.envsci.rutgers.edu/climdyn2017/LorenzButterfly.pdf). It was a question, not an experimentally demonstrated butterfly–tornado chain.

- **High as a dated finding — The 2019 practical benchmark was about 9–10 days.** Zhang and colleagues describe practical predictability as **“about 9–10 days.”** [Zhang et al., 2019, conclusions](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/atsc/76/4/jas-d-18-0269.1.xml). This concerns their assessment of midlatitude day-to-day weather, not every forecast and not a universal 2026 boundary.

- **Medium as a general limit; high as the paper’s result — An intrinsic limit near two weeks.** Their high-resolution experiments suggest **“about 2 weeks.”** [Zhang et al., 2019](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/atsc/76/4/jas-d-18-0269.1.xml). Present this as research evidence for an approximate intrinsic limit, rather than an exact theorem applying equally to all atmospheric quantities.

- **High within those experiments — Better initial conditions bought additional days.** Reducing initial-condition uncertainty by an **order of magnitude** extended deterministic lead time by **“up to 5 days.”** [Zhang et al., 2019, abstract](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/atsc/76/4/jas-d-18-0269.1.xml). That is a model-experiment result, not a promise that a particular observing upgrade will deliver five days.

- **High — A skill threshold is not a percentage of forecasts correct.** The paper uses an anomaly correlation coefficient for **500-hPa geopotential height**, with **“60%”** as a conventional threshold for useful synoptic-scale skill. [Zhang et al., 2019, p. 1077](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/20595/noaa_20595_DS1.pdf). Do not translate this into “weather forecasts are 60% accurate.”

- **High — Ensembles represent alternative plausible evolutions.** ECMWF’s physics-based ensemble currently comprises **51 forecasts: one control plus 50 perturbed members**, with altered starting conditions and model physics. **Exact excerpts:** “51 forecasts”; “one control forecast (CNTL), plus 50.” [ECMWF, *Medium-range forecasts*](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/forecasts/documentation-and-support/medium-range-forecasts). Ensemble spread is information about uncertainty, not a guarantee that reality must fall inside every displayed range.

- **High — Operational products already extend to fifteen days.** **Exact heading:** “Forecasts up to 15 days ahead.” [ECMWF](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/forecasts/documentation-and-support/medium-range-forecasts). Product length does not imply equal confidence on every day, but it directly disproves a literal ten-day stopping rule.

- **High as a documented historical trend — Skill improved by about one day per decade.** ECMWF’s 2010 account reports **“around one day per decade.”** For its large-scale 500-hPa anomaly-correlation measure, the 60% threshold shifted from **“around forecast day 5”** in the early 1980s to **“around day 8”** on average by 2010. [ECMWF, 12 March 2010](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/about/media-centre/news/2010/landmark-forecast-performance). This is a historical, metric-specific trend, not an indefinitely valid linear law.

- **High — Longer-range predictions address different questions.** ECMWF issues sub-seasonal predictions **“up to 46 days ahead”**, often for temperature **“averaged over seven days.”** [ECMWF fact sheet, 22 July 2025](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/about/media-centre/focus/2025/fact-sheet-sub-seasonal-and-seasonal-predictions). Predicting a week’s tendency to be warmer than normal differs from predicting the precise weather at a particular hour.

**3. Retellings to avoid**

- “Everything after day ten is worthless.”
- “Scientists proved an exact fourteen-day limit for all weather.”
- “A butterfly creates a tornado.” Lorenz explicitly left the literal question open: **“leave our original question unanswered.”** [1972 talk](https://climate.envsci.rutgers.edu/climdyn2017/LorenzButterfly.pdf).
- “Lorenz rounded a temperature by 0.000127 degrees.” The familiar account does not justify that unit.
- Portraying the 1961 experiment as the same three-equation system published in 1963 without checking the distinction.
- Treating ensemble spread or a skill score as a simple “percentage chance the forecast is correct.”
- Using limited detailed weather predictability to claim seasonal averages cannot be predicted.

**4. Best hook**

“A forecast can lose the future in its last decimal places. Tiny starting errors grow into different weather.”
