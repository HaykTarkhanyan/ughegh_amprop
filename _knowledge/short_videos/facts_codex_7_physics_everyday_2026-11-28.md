## P2. Do mirrors really flip left and right?

### 1. Verdict on the premise

**High confidence: the proposed correction is accurate, with a coordinate-system qualification.** A plane mirror reverses the coordinate **perpendicular to its surface** and preserves coordinates parallel to it. For someone facing an upright mirror, that means front–back, rather than room-left–room-right or up–down.

The familiar left–right description comes from comparing the reflection with an upright person who has turned to face us. Present this as an explanation of the comparison, rather than a claim that every observer performs a particular unconscious mental operation.

### 2. Key facts

- **High — The precise transformation is \((x,y,z)\rightarrow(x,y,-z)\), when the mirror occupies the \(xy\)-plane.** This is the cleanest formulation because it remains correct when the mirror or person changes orientation. **Quoted evidence:** “the coordinates of the virtual image point I′ are (X, Y, −Z).” [PPLATO/FLAP, §3.2, hosted by Brock University](https://www.physics.brocku.ca/PPLATO/h-flap/phys6_2.html).

- **High — In a fixed room coordinate system, the mirror does not exchange its two sideways directions.** **Quoted evidence:** “Mirrors do not flip left to right. They flip front to back.” [Christopher S. Baird, West Texas A&M University, 2013](https://www.wtamu.edu/~cbaird/sq/2013/01/05/why-do-mirrors-flip-left-to-right-and-not-up-to-down/index.html).

- **High — Moving a hand sideways provides a direct demonstration.** The reflected hand moves in the same sideways direction; moving toward the mirror instead makes object and image approach from opposite directions. **Quoted evidence:** “the mirror image moves in the same direction”; “the image moves in the opposite direction!” [Purdue University, demonstration 7A-03](https://www.physics.purdue.edu/demos/display_page.php?item=7A-03).

- **High for the geometry; medium as a universal psychological explanation — An upright comparison person turns around a vertical axis.** That comparison exchanges sideways positions while preserving head–feet orientation. **Quoted evidence:** “pivoting on one’s vertical axis”. Block discusses this explicitly as one interpretation of the puzzle, rather than its only possible meaning. [Ned Block, *Why Do Mirrors Reverse Right/Left but Not Up/Down?*, 1974](https://home.sandiego.edu/~baber/metaphysics/readings/Block.Mirrors.pdf).

- **High — Reflection really changes handedness.** Saying “no left–right coordinate exchange” does not mean the image is identical to the original under rotation. **Quoted evidence:** “no matter how you rotate your right hand it never becomes a left hand”. [PPLATO/FLAP, discussion of Figure 6](https://www.physics.brocku.ca/PPLATO/h-flap/phys6_2.html).

- **High — Turning a printed sheet toward a mirror contributes to the apparent reversal.** Purdue’s transparency demonstration removes the opaque backing so viewers can compare the actual lettering with its reflection without another turn. **Quoted evidence:** “rotated the image 180 degrees!” [Purdue University, demonstration 7A-03](https://www.physics.purdue.edu/demos/display_page.php?item=7A-03).

- **High — An ideal plane mirror produces an upright, same-size virtual image at the same perpendicular distance behind the mirror as the object is in front.** **Quoted evidence:** “same size as the object”; “cannot be projected onto a screen”; equation \(d_\mathrm{o}=-d_\mathrm{i}\). [Ling, Sanny and Moebs, *University Physics*, vol. 3, §2.1, 2016](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-3/pages/2-1-images-formed-by-plane-mirrors).

- **High — Some ambulances carry mirror-written front lettering so drivers ahead can read it in a rear-view mirror.** This is a practical application of reflection, not evidence for a special horizontal optical rule. **Quoted evidence:** “see the writing the correct way round.” [Institute of Physics, “Reading in reverse”](https://spark.iop.org/collections/reflection-and-refraction-physics-narrative).

- **High — Two plane mirrors meeting at 90° can produce a non-reversing double-reflection image.** With a vertical seam, the two reflections together are geometrically equivalent to a **180° rotation about that seam**—a deduction from composing the reflections. **Quoted evidence:** “produce an image which is not reversed”. [Klaus-Dieter Graf and Bernard R. Hodgson, 1990, p. 44](https://flm-journal.org/Articles/1E8C6E349DC5EDF9FFFDCDCA6E79.pdf).

- **High — The corner arrangement can also show ordinary single-reflection images.** Identify the central, twice-reflected image when demonstrating the non-reversing effect. **Quoted evidence:** “two mirrors at right angles form three images”. [OpenStax, §2.1, Figure 2.4](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-3/pages/2-1-images-formed-by-plane-mirrors).

### 3. Retellings to avoid

- **“A mirror never reverses up and down.”** The invariant rule concerns the surface normal. A horizontal mirror reverses the vertical coordinate.
- **“Nothing is reversed; it is all an illusion.”** Handedness changes physically in the image geometry.
- **“The brain always rotates the image.”** Treat rotation as the comparison being made, not a universally established mental mechanism.
- **“Two reflections always restore the original image.”** Their combined effect depends on mirror orientation.
- **“A corner mirror shows exactly what everyone else sees.”** It restores ordinary handedness; viewpoint, perspective and lighting still matter.
- For **AMBULANCE**, mirror the actual glyphs as well as their order. Merely typing the letters backward is not a complete mirror image.

These corrections follow the coordinate rule and demonstrations in facts 1–10.

### 4. Best hook

> “Mirrors don’t swap left and right. They reverse the direction straight through the mirror.”

**Suggested small source line:** C. S. Baird, 2013; Graf & Hodgson, 1990.

### 5. Showcase idea

Give a person three coloured arrows: sideways, upward and toward the mirror. Reveal the reflected arrows: only the perpendicular arrow reverses. Then unfold a second mirror to **90°** and reveal readable lettering in the twice-reflected image.

Use “room-left” and “the reflected person’s anatomical left” as distinct labels. The **180°** combined rotation is a geometric derivation, not a measured biological effect.

## P4. How a microwave oven heats food

### 1. Verdict on the premise

**High confidence: the mechanism is dielectric heating, and the water-resonance story is false.** The changing electric field acts on molecular dipoles, particularly water, and electrical losses transfer energy into heat. Dissolved ions can also contribute.

**2.45 GHz is a nominal operating frequency, not water’s special resonant frequency.** Its suitability involves useful penetration and an allocated industrial, scientific and medical—ISM—band.

### 2. Key facts

- **High — Most conventional domestic ovens operate nominally at 2.45 GHz.** This is **2,450 MHz**, not the mains electricity frequency. **Quoted evidence:** “2450 MHz (megahertz) for most conventional ovens”. [Canadian Centre for Occupational Health and Safety, revised 2024](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/phys_agents/microwave_ovens.html).

- **High — The corresponding free-space wavelength is about 12.2 cm.** **Quoted evidence:** NIST gives \(c=\) “299 792 458 m s⁻¹”, with uncertainty “(exact)”. **Calculation:** \(\lambda=c/f=0.122364…\ \mathrm m\), or approximately **12.24 cm**. This is not the wavelength inside every food. [NIST, 2022 CODATA value](https://physics.nist.gov/cgi-bin/cuu/Value?c%7Csearch_for=c).

- **High — The electric field exerts a turning influence on molecular dipoles.** Their response and interactions dissipate energy as heat; the animation should show attempted alignment and lag, not isolated water molecules spinning freely like propellers. **Quoted evidence:** “causing them to rotate in order to align with the field”. [Christopher S. Baird, West Texas A&M University, 2014](https://www.wtamu.edu/~cbaird/sq/2014/10/15/why-are-the-microwaves-in-a-microwave-oven-tuned-to-water/).

- **High — 2.45 GHz is not a special water resonance.** **Quoted evidence:** “not tuned to a resonant frequency of water”. Separately, measured dielectric-loss spectra show a broad feature at “about 20 GHz”—not a sharp 2.45-GHz molecular resonance. The feature’s position depends on temperature. [Baird, 2014](https://www.wtamu.edu/~cbaird/sq/2014/10/15/why-are-the-microwaves-in-a-microwave-oven-tuned-to-water/); [P. Lunkenheimer and colleagues, 2016 preprint, revised 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01457).

- **High for the allocation and engineering explanation; medium for a complete historical selection story — The frequency combines an ISM allocation with useful penetration.** Stronger absorption at higher frequencies can deposit energy too close to the surface. **Quoted evidence:** FCC table row “2450 MHz | ± 50.0 MHz”; Vollmer: “the penetration depth should be in this range”, referring to centimetre-scale food dimensions. [47 CFR §18.301, 2025 edition, p. 1129](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2025-title47-vol1/pdf/CFR-2025-title47-vol1-part18.pdf); [Michael Vollmer, 2004, p. 79](https://users.wfu.edu/natalie/s24phy712/lecturenote/physics_of_microwave_ovens.pdf).

- **High — Reflections inside the metal cavity create standing-wave patterns and uneven heating.** A turntable improves averaging by moving food through different field regions; it does not guarantee uniform temperature. **Quoted evidence:** “move the food in and out of the hot spots.” Some designs instead use a mode stirrer. [Vollmer, 2004, p. 77](https://users.wfu.edu/natalie/s24phy712/lecturenote/physics_of_microwave_ovens.pdf).

- **High — Ice absorbs microwave energy much less effectively than liquid water under ordinary oven conditions.** Its molecular arrangement restricts the dipoles’ response; melted regions can consequently heat faster than neighbouring frozen regions. **Quoted evidence:** “in a crystal lattice”. [Vollmer, 2004, pp. 79–80](https://users.wfu.edu/natalie/s24phy712/lecturenote/physics_of_microwave_ovens.pdf).

- **High for the simple standing-wave model — Adjacent heating antinodes are half a wavelength apart.** **Quoted evidence:** “The distance between neighboring antinodes is equal to one-half of the wavelength.” **Calculation:** at 2.45 GHz, the ideal free-space half-wavelength is approximately **6.12 cm**. [Jefferson Lab, chocolate experiment transcript](https://stem-tools.jlab.org/frost/speed_of_light).

- **High — Chocolate can estimate wave speed, but a kitchen experiment is not automatically precise.** Use \(v=f\lambda=2fd\), where \(d\) is the inferred adjacent-antinode spacing. Jefferson Lab measured **7.1 cm**, obtained approximately **\(3.5\times10^8\) m/s**, and reported “17% error”. Real cavity geometry, loading and heat spreading complicate the simple model. [Jefferson Lab’s actual demonstration](https://stem-tools.jlab.org/frost/speed_of_light).

- **High — Spencer’s documented patent timeline is 1945 filing and 1950 grant.** The record names **Percy L. Spencer**, with **Raytheon Manufacturing Co.** as original assignee. **Quoted evidence:** filing “1945-10-08”; “1950-01-24 Application granted”. [US2495429A, *Method of treating foodstuffs*](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2495429A/en); [original patent scan](https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/0c/fe/fb/c488f9692f4f8d/US2495429.pdf).

- **Medium — The melted-candy-bar episode is a widely reported anecdote, not equivalently strong evidence.** MIT recounts that Spencer “noticed that the chocolate bar in his pocket had started to melt”. I found no contemporaneous laboratory note or eyewitness document establishing the precise episode or candy brand. Introduce it as a reported story. [Lemelson-MIT, Percy Spencer biography](https://lemelson.mit.edu/resources/percy-spencer).

- **High — Microwave ovens do not generally cook thick food from the centre outward.** Direct heating occurs over a limited penetration region, while conduction helps heat deeper parts. **Quoted evidence:** “microwave ovens do not cook food from the ‘inside out.’” [US Food and Drug Administration](https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/resources-you-radiation-emitting-products/microwave-ovens).

### 3. Retellings to avoid

- **“2.45 GHz is water’s resonant frequency.”** False; distinguish dielectric relaxation from a sharp molecular resonance.
- **“Only water heats.”** False. Other dielectric constituents and dissolved ions can absorb energy. [Vollmer, 2004, p. 79](https://users.wfu.edu/natalie/s24phy712/lecturenote/physics_of_microwave_ovens.pdf).
- **“Every molecule completes 2.45 billion rotations per second.”** The field oscillation rate does not establish that motion.
- **“Hot spots are always exactly 6 cm apart.”** That is a simplified model prediction, not a universal measured oven pattern.
- **“Spencer received the microwave-cooking patent in 1946.”** A university teaching brief says “patented in 1946”, but the original patent establishes the filing and grant dates above. **Trust the patent for patent dates.** [University of Michigan brief](https://em8e.eecs.umich.edu/pdf/tb3.pdf).
- **“Microwaving makes food radioactive.”** False. FDA states that it “does not make food ‘radioactive’”. [FDA](https://www.fda.gov/radiation-emitting-products/resources-you-radiation-emitting-products/microwave-ovens).

### 4. Best hook

> “Your microwave has invisible hot spots. The spinning plate moves your food through them.”

**Suggested small source line:** Michael Vollmer, 2004; P. Lunkenheimer et al., 2017; Spencer, US2495429, filed 1945.

### 5. Showcase idea

Reveal an ideal standing electric-field pattern above stationary chocolate. Highlight heating at **both kinds of antinode**, separated by approximately **6.12 cm** in the simple model. Label the full wavelength **12.24 cm** and frequency **2.45 GHz**.

Then rotate the food through the pattern. If including the light-speed extension, label it **“approximate measurement”** and show actual measured spacing, rather than silently substituting the theoretical value.

- **High — [File:NS Savannah microwave oven MD8.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:NS_Savannah_microwave_oven_MD8.jpg)** — Acroterion; **CC BY-SA 4.0 International**, checked on Commons. Licence evidence: “Attribution-Share Alike 4.0 International”. Caption it as the Raytheon oven aboard *NS Savannah*, not as Spencer’s original experimental apparatus.

### 7. Armenian anchor

### 8. Open questions

- The candy brand, exact day and laboratory circumstances remain **unverified**.
- **Commercial-launch dates disagree:** APS says “Radarange hit the market in 1946”; MIT says “marketed in 1947”. Neither passage resolves the discrepancy with contemporary sales documentation. Omit the launch year unless separately researched; the patent dates are secure. [Emily Conover, APS, 2015](https://www.aps.org/apsnews/2015/10/first-patent-for-microwave); [Lemelson-MIT](https://lemelson.mit.edu/resources/percy-spencer).

- A specific oven’s chocolate/cheese spacing and measurement error must be measured; neither can be guaranteed beforehand.

## I4. Why batteries die in the cold

### 1. Verdict on the premise

**High confidence, if “die” means temporarily lose usable power or trigger shutdown.** Cold slows transport and electrochemical processes and increases impedance. A battery can therefore have charge remaining while being unable to maintain the voltage a device needs.

Ordinary cold-related performance loss can recover on warming. **Charging a cold cell is a separate issue:** lithium plating can cause lasting damage. Avoid promising that all effects of cold are reversible.

### 2. Key facts

- **High — Cold impairs electrochemical kinetics and reduces deliverable power and capacity.** An original impedance study links charge-transfer resistance to reaction kinetics and reports that “both the power (operating voltage) and energy (delivered capacity) are substantially reduced.” [S. S. Zhang, K. Xu and T. R. Jow, 2004](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0013468603008478).

- **High — Electrolyte viscosity rises and ion transport becomes slower.** This is one contributor, rather than the whole explanation. **Quoted evidence:** “more slowly due to increasing viscosity”. Hickson and colleagues measured temperature-dependent electrolyte transport; their investigated formulation should not be treated as a numerical model of every phone battery. [Darby Hickson and colleagues, 2024, abstract](https://escholarship.org/content/qt8bd0h5sc/qt8bd0h5sc.pdf).

- **High — Increased impedance can produce voltage sag and shutdown under load.** Apple identifies cold as a contributor, explains that voltage “will drop to a greater degree”, and that the system can “perform a shutdown”. This supports shutdown with charge remaining, not sudden disappearance of all stored energy. [Apple, *iPhone battery and performance*](https://support.apple.com/en-us/101575).

- **High — Apple specifies an ambient operating range of 0–35°C for iPhone and iPad.** **Quoted evidence:** “between 0º and 35º C”. This is a device specification, not a universal lithium-ion chemistry boundary or a promise of shutdown precisely at 0°C. [Apple Support, published 14 May 2026](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118431).

- **High — Apple explicitly describes cold-related battery-life reduction as temporary.** **Quoted evidence:** “Battery life will return to normal”. Warming can restore access to remaining charge; it does not replace energy already consumed. [Apple Support, “If your device gets too cold”](https://support.apple.com/en-us/118431).

- **High — Cold charging favours competition between insertion into graphite and deposition of metallic lithium.** Plating can reduce capacity and create damaging deposits. **Quoted evidence:** “especially at low temperatures and fast rates”; “formation of Li that becomes electrochemically inactive”. [Brian Chen and colleagues, original experimental study, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12051203/).

- **High for the risk; medium for any blanket cutoff — Below 0°C is a useful caution for conventional graphite-anode cells, not a universal on/off plating threshold.** Rate, state of charge, design and actual cell temperature matter. **Quoted evidence:** “sub-zero conditions”; “lithium deposition at low anode potentials”. Follow the product’s charging limits rather than deriving permission from air temperature alone. [Dion Hubble and colleagues, 2022, pp. 556–557 and 573](https://escholarship.org/content/qt3rq1k2zq/qt3rq1k2zq_noSplash_8342fbc020f6415358a72ef558a66f43.pdf).

- **High — AAA’s 2019 results concern a controlled test of five vehicles.** They are not a universal percentage for modern EVs. **Quoted evidence:** “five (5) battery electric vehicles (BEVs)”; tested temperatures “20°F, 75°F and 95°F”. Tests used a climate-controlled chassis dynamometer. [AAA, February 2019 report, p. 3 and methodology](https://www.aaa.com/AAA/common/AAR/files/AAA-Electric-Vehicle-Range-Testing-Report.pdf).

- **High — At 20°F, approximately −6.7°C, AAA found average combined range reductions of 12% without HVAC and 41% with heating.** Both comparisons used the **75°F, approximately 23.9°C, HVAC-off baseline**. **Quoted evidence:** “12 percent decrease”; “41 percent decrease”. Celsius values are conversions. These figures include vehicle operation and heating effects, not simply loss of battery capacity. [AAA, pp. 3 and 33](https://www.aaa.com/AAA/common/AAR/files/AAA-Electric-Vehicle-Range-Testing-Report.pdf).

- **High within its product scope — Energizer does not recommend refrigeration for its ordinary non-rechargeable batteries.** **Quoted evidence:** “storage in a refrigerator or freezer is not required or recommended”. Its explanation includes possible condensation-related corrosion or damage. Do not misidentify this as a lithium-ion-specific study. [Energizer, FAQ, 2012, question 1](https://data.energizer.com/pdfs/non-rechargeable_faq.pdf).

- **High — Apple’s long-term lithium-ion storage guidance is partial charge and a cool, dry environment.** **Quoted evidence:** “charge it to around 50%”; “a cool, moisture-free environment”. This supports sensible cool storage, not routine household refrigeration. [Apple, *Maximizing Battery Life and Lifespan*](https://www.apple.com/batteries/maximizing-performance/).

### 3. Retellings to avoid

- **“Cold permanently destroys the missing percentage.”** Temporary available capacity and permanent degradation are different.
- **“Warming recharges the battery.”** It can restore performance and access to remaining energy.
- **“The electrolyte freezes at 0°C like water.”** Lithium-ion electrolytes are formulated mixtures; 0°C is not their universal freezing point.
- **“The only problem is thicker electrolyte.”** Interfaces, reaction kinetics and transport within electrodes also matter.
- **“Every EV loses 41% in winter.”** That is AAA’s particular 2019 test average with heating.
- **“Below 0°C, plugging in any EV inevitably damages it.”** Charging controls and battery heating change the cell’s conditions; the relevant risk concerns the actual cell and charging regime.
- **“Cold storage is always bad” / “the fridge always extends battery life.”** Both erase chemistry and storage-condition differences.

### 4. Best hook

> “Your phone can ‘die’ without being empty. In the cold, its battery may have charge it cannot deliver fast enough.”

**Suggested small source line:** Zhang, Xu & Jow, 2004; Hickson et al., 2024; AAA, 2019; Apple, 2026.

### 5. Showcase idea

Animate a battery powering a phone. As it cools, show slower ion transport and a greater voltage drop under load until the phone shuts down. Keep remaining charge visible; label the animation **schematic**, without invented percentages or voltage thresholds.

For the extension, show a range comparison normalised to **100 units**:

| AAA test condition | Relative range |
|---|---:|
| 75°F / 23.9°C, HVAC off | 100 |
| 20°F / −6.7°C, HVAC off | 88 |
| 20°F / −6.7°C, heating on | 59 |

The **88** and **59** are arithmetic restatements of AAA’s **12%** and **41%** reductions, not additional measurements. Put **“AAA, 2019; five vehicles”** on screen.

### 8. Open questions

- There is **no verified universal shutdown temperature**, capacity-loss percentage or warming-recovery time for all phones.
- “Mostly recovered” cannot be assigned a universal percentage: chemistry, age, load and prior treatment differ.
- AAA’s older controlled sample does not establish present-day fleet-wide winter losses. A particular current model needs its own test.
- A specific battery’s safe cold-charging boundary must come from its manufacturer or validated charging protocol.

## CX40. How ships climb a hill

### 1. Verdict on the premise

**High confidence: locks let vessels gain elevation while remaining afloat.** A conventional pound lock changes the water level inside a fixed chamber; the ship rises or falls with that water. Water flows from a higher level to a lower one under gravity.

A boat lift is the useful extension: it moves the vessel, often together with its water-filled chamber. Avoid treating locks and mechanical lifts as the same machine.

### 2. Key facts

- **High — A conventional lock raises or lowers boats by filling or emptying a chamber.** Gates open for passage after water levels equalise. **Quoted evidence:** “Boats are raised or lowered by filling or emptying the lock chamber.” [Parks Canada, Lachine Canal, updated 2024](https://www.parcs.canada.ca/lhn-nhs/qc/canallachine/culture/ingenierie-engineering/ecluse-fonction-lock).

- **High — Gravity can fill and empty the chamber without pumping that operating water uphill.** Gate machinery may still use electricity or hydraulics. **Quoted evidence:** “by gravity using the communicating vessels principle.” [Parks Canada](https://www.parcs.canada.ca/lhn-nhs/qc/canallachine/culture/ingenierie-engineering/ecluse-fonction-lock).

- **Medium — Qiao Weiyue is credited with a Chinese pound lock around 984 CE.** Koenig and Kink, citing Needham, specify “around 984 by the official Qiao Weiyue” and “without miter gates”. **Disagreement:** Canal & River Trust calls the first two-ended lock one “built in Holland in 1373”. **Trust the specialist history for the global attribution**; the operator’s simplified history omits the earlier Chinese evidence. [Albert Koenig and Sabine Kink, 2022, p. 88](https://files.sciengine.com/pdfs/2096-4226/6D62DFBF3D264E40994396A21AB7BEA5.pdf); [Canal & River Trust](https://canalrivertrust.org.uk/canals-and-rivers/how-does-a-canal-lock-work).

- **High for Leonardo’s surviving design; medium for sole-inventor credit — Leonardo drew a mitre-gate lock design.** Linda Hall Library identifies a design for San Marco, completed in **1497**: “details for the miter gate”. The Trust instead says “Leonardo da Vinci invented the double mitre lock”. **Prefer the narrower, demonstrable claim about his drawing** over exclusive invention credit. [Linda Hall Library, original drawing and historical explanation](https://www.lindahall.org/experience/digital-exhibitions/centuries-of-civil-engineering/01-canals/); [Canal & River Trust](https://canalrivertrust.org.uk/canals-and-rivers/how-does-a-canal-lock-work).

- **High — Panama Canal locks raise vessels to Gatun Lake, approximately 26 m above sea level.** Their operating water comes from the lake under gravity. **Quoted evidence:** “26 meters above sea level”; “obtained from Gatun Lake by gravity”. [Panama Canal Authority, operating explanation](https://pancanal.com/en/history-of-the-panama-canal/).

- **High — The original Panama route has three steps at Gatun, one at Pedro Miguel and two at Miraflores.** The original chambers are arranged in paired lanes. **Quoted evidence:** “Gatun Locks consists of three steps”; “one step at Pedro Miguel and two at Miraflores”. [Panama Canal Authority, *Design of the Locks*](https://pancanal.com/en/design-of-the-locks/).

- **High as attributed approximate figures; medium as a single universal consumption number — ACP water-use estimates differ.** A **2002** statement gives “about 52 million gallons” per transit. Its current Spanish FAQ gives Panamax “unos 55 millones de galones” and Neopanamax with basins “unos 51 millones de galones”. **Prefer the configuration-specific FAQ for those comparisons**, while retaining 52 million only as a dated approximate statement. These are transit figures, not water use for every individual chamber. [ACP, 2002](https://pancanal.com/en/panama-canal-authority-concludes-consultation-process-and-announces-final-tolls-structure/); [ACP FAQ](https://pancanal.com/preguntas-frecuentes/).

- **High — The new Agua Clara and Cocolí locks have three chambers per complex and three saving basins per chamber: 18 basins altogether.** ACP reports **60% water reuse** and approximately **7% less water use than the original locks**, despite the larger chambers. **Quoted evidence:** “18 basins”; “reuse 60 percent”; “saving 7 percent more”. The percentages have different comparison bases. [Panama Canal Authority, 5 February 2016](https://pancanal.com/en/first-panama-canal-water-saving-basin-filled-testing-process-begins/).

- **High for opening year; medium pending operator-only confirmation of lift height — The Falkirk Wheel opened in 2002; its mechanical lift is approximately 24 m.** Scottish Canals says “opened in 2002” but loosely describes carrying barges “35 metres into the air”. Structural designer Tony Gee specifies the “24m vertical step”; ICE independently identifies the aqueduct as **24 m** above the lower canal. **Trust the detailed engineering description for lift height.** I did not verify 24 m on the operator’s current page. [Scottish Canals](https://www.scottishcanals.co.uk/visit/canals/visit-the-forth-clyde-canal/attractions/the-falkirk-wheel); [Tony Gee](https://tonygee.com/our-work/falkirk-wheel); [Institution of Civil Engineers](https://ice.org.uk/what-is-civil-engineering/infrastructure-projects/falkirk-wheel).

- **High — A floating boat need not make a correctly filled gondola heavier.** It displaces its own weight of water; at the controlled water level, water leaves as the boat enters. **Quoted evidence:** “‘boat plus water’ balances the original total mass.” This is the balancing principle, not a claim of zero friction or zero energy consumption. [Tony Gee, structural designer](https://tonygee.com/our-work/falkirk-wheel).

- **Medium — Scottish Canals publishes approximately 1.5 kWh for an operating rotation.** **Quoted evidence:** “1.5kWh for each rotation.” The page does not clearly define whether “rotation” means the operating half-turn or a full revolution. **Do not claim a verified 360° energy figure.** [Scottish Canals](https://www.scottishcanals.co.uk/visit/canals/visit-the-forth-clyde-canal/attractions/the-falkirk-wheel).

- **High for the engineering dimensions and dated operator statement — Three Gorges’ ship lift can accommodate 3,000-tonne vessels and lift through a maximum 113 m.** CTG’s **2024** account gives “3,000-tonne vessels” and “113 meters”, and calls it the “world’s largest ship lift”. Its designer explicitly states “maximum lifting height of 113 m”. Attribute the superlative to the operator; 113 m is a maximum, not every trip’s rise. [China Three Gorges Corporation, 22 May 2024](https://www.ctg.com.cn/ctgenglish/news_media/news37/2024080621171694810/index.html); [Krebs+Kiefer engineering brochure](https://www.kuk.de/fileadmin/user_upload/broschueren/kk-broschuere-schiffshebewerke.pdf).

### 3. Retellings to avoid

- **“The ship is pushed up a sloping sheet of water.”** A lock provides successive level stages.
- **“No energy is needed.”** Gravity-fed locks consume elevated water; mechanical lifts also have losses.
- **“All canals operate without pumps.”** The chamber’s gravity operation does not establish how the wider water supply is maintained.
- **“Leonardo invented locks.”** Earlier pound locks are documented; his gate drawings are the secure connection.
- **“The new Panama locks use 60% less water than the old locks.”** ACP’s old-versus-new comparison is approximately **7%**; **60%** describes reuse.
- **“Every Panama passage always consumes exactly 52 million gallons.”** ACP itself gives differing approximate, configuration-specific figures.
- **“The Falkirk Wheel lifts boats 35 m.”** That confuses the overall canal connection with the approximately **24 m** mechanical step.
- **“A boat’s weight is irrelevant under every circumstance.”** Equal balance depends on flotation and controlled water levels.
- **“Three Gorges lifts a 3,000-tonne boat 113 m on every journey.”** Both are capacity limits.

### 4. Best hook

> “Ships can climb using falling water. A lock raises the water—and the ship floats up with it.”

**Suggested small source line:** Koenig & Kink, 2022; Panama Canal Authority, 2016; Scottish Canals; CTG, 2024.

### 5. Showcase idea

Show one transparent lock chamber:

1. A boat enters at the lower level.
2. Gates close; water enters from the upper reach through a culvert.
3. The boat floats upward.
4. Once levels match, the upper gates open.

Then morph the chamber into a Falkirk gondola. As a boat enters, animate an **equal weight of water** leaving; the balance remains level. This gives one core idea—changing elevation while floating—and one extension—moving a balanced chamber.

If adding a Panama scale label, use **“Gatun Lake: about 26 m above sea level”**. Avoid assigning an invented height, fill time or water volume to the generic lock.
