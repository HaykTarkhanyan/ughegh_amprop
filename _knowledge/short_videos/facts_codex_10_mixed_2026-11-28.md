# «Ուղեղ Ամպրոպ» — Research fact sheets

**Confidence:** **high** = directly supported or well established; **medium** = interpretation, qualified evidence, or incomplete primary-source access; **low** = not adequately verified. Quoted evidence below consists of short verbatim passages or numerical entries. Calculations are identified explicitly.

## X4. Six degrees of separation

**1. Verdict on the premise**

**Not established as a universal rule.** Experiments found short *successful* acquaintance chains, while many chains failed. Their results do not prove that every pair of humans can find each other through six handshakes.

Accurate wording: **“Some surprisingly distant people can be connected through a short chain of acquaintances. Measuring those connections—and finding them—are different problems.”**

**2. Key facts**

- **High — Karinthy’s contribution was fiction, not experimental evidence.** Frigyes Karinthy’s *Láncszemek*, translated as *Chains* or *Chain-Links*, appeared in **1929** in *Everything Is Different*. A character proposes reaching a selected person through at most five individuals. **Quoted evidence:** “(1929, Everything is Different)”; “using no more than five individuals, one of whom is a personal acquaintance”. [Story, translated by Adam Makkai, pp. 1–2](https://users.dimi.uniud.it/~massimo.franceschet/ns/syllabus/bibliography/Karinthy-Chain-Links_1929.pdf).

- **High — Wichita and Omaha belong to different trials.** The Wichita, Kansas trial targeted a divinity student’s wife in **Cambridge**. The Nebraska/Omaha trial targeted a stockbroker who worked in **Boston** and lived in **Sharon, Massachusetts**. Do not merge them into one experiment with one target. **Quoted evidence:** “Wichita, Kansas”; “wife of a divinity student living in Cambridge”; “volunteers in Omaha, Nebraska”. [Kleinfeld, 2002, pp. 61–63](https://www.csc2.ncsu.edu/faculty/mpsingh/local/Social/f25/wrap/readings/Kleinfeld-2002-18673541.pdf), [historical account, footnote 86](https://ctlj.colorado.edu/wp-content/uploads/2019/03/5-Yoo_3.18.19.pdf).

- **High — The familiar 296-person sample is documented in Travers and Milgram’s 1969 paper.** It comprised **196 Nebraska volunteers and 100 Boston-area volunteers**, directed toward a Boston stockbroker. **Quoted entries:** “296”, “196”, “100”; “Boston stockbroker”. [Travers & Milgram, 1969, p. 429](https://people.sabanciuniv.edu/atilgan/CEM-Spring17/CasualReading/Milgram_Sociometry69_SmallWorld.pdf).

- **High — Distinguish recruitment, forwarding, and arrival.** Of those volunteers, **217 actually forwarded the document; 64 chains reached the target**. The successful chains averaged **5.2 intermediaries**. **Quoted evidence:** “217”; “64”; “Within this group the mean number of intermediaries between starters and targets is 5.2.” [Travers & Milgram, abstract and p. 431](https://people.sabanciuniv.edu/atilgan/CEM-Spring17/CasualReading/Milgram_Sociometry69_SmallWorld.pdf).

- **High — Completion percentages depend on the denominator.** From the quoted counts above, **64/296 = 21.6%** of recruited starters produced completed chains; **64/217 = 29.5%** of chains actually forwarded were completed. These are **calculations**, rounded to one decimal place. [Underlying counts](https://people.sabanciuniv.edu/atilgan/CEM-Spring17/CasualReading/Milgram_Sociometry69_SmallWorld.pdf).

- **High for her findings; medium for broader conclusions — Kleinfeld’s 2002 critique concerned missing results, sampling, and attrition.** Her archival account reports **60 recruited Wichita starters, 50 initiated chains, and 3 arrivals**: **5% of recruits**, not 5% of initiated chains. She also criticized the striking Kansas anecdote being published without the trial’s full results and recruitment favouring sociable participants. **Quoted evidence:** “50”; “3”; “60”; “(5%)”; “without giving the statistical results of this study.” [Kleinfeld, 2002, pp. 62–63](https://www.csc2.ncsu.edu/faculty/mpsingh/local/Social/f25/wrap/readings/Kleinfeld-2002-18673541.pdf).

- **High — The 2003 email experiment also had substantial attrition.** Peter Sheridan Dodds, Roby Muhamad and Duncan Watts recorded **61,168 participants in 166 countries**, forming **24,163 chains**, aimed at **18 targets in 13 countries**. **384 chains arrived**—approximately **1.59%**, calculated. **Quoted numerical entries:** “61,168”, “166”, “24,163”, “18”, “13”, “384”. [Dodds, Muhamad & Watts, 2003, pp. 827–828](https://pdodds.w3.uvm.edu/research/papers/years/2003/dodds2003b.pdf).

- **High for reported estimates; medium as a general description of humanity — “Five to seven” was an attrition-adjusted estimate.** Completed email chains averaged **4.05** in the paper’s chain-length measure. Assuming random attrition, the researchers estimated a typical **median of five to seven steps**, depending on source–target geography. This was not the directly observed average of all attempts. **Quoted evidence:** “4.05”; “random attrition”; “five to seven steps”. [Dodds et al., 2003, p. 828](https://pdodds.w3.uvm.edu/research/papers/years/2003/dodds2003b.pdf).

- **High — Watts and Strogatz’s 1998 result describes network structure.** Their model showed how strong local clustering can coexist with short paths after some connections are rewired. It does not prove a fixed six-step limit for humans. **Quoted sentence:** “We find that these systems can be highly clustered, like regular lattices, yet have small characteristic path lengths, like random graphs.” [Watts & Strogatz, 1998, p. 440](https://gwern.net/doc/science/physics/1998-watts.pdf).

- **High — Backstrom et al.’s 2012 paper used a May 2011 Facebook snapshot.** Approximately **721 million active users** and **69 billion friendship links** yielded an estimated mean shortest-path distance of **4.74 hops**, equivalent to **3.74 intermediate people**. **Quoted entries:** “May 2011”; “721 million”; “69 billion”; “4.74”; “3.74”. [Backstrom, Boldi, Rosa, Ugander & Vigna, abstract and §4](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~mkearns/teaching/NetworkedLife/Backstrom.pdf).

- **Medium — Facebook’s 2016 announcement reported 3.57 intermediate people.** Its stated population was approximately **1.59 billion active users**. The corresponding hop distance is **4.57**, calculated by adding one. **Quoted graph label:** “Mean = 3.57”. The original post currently redirects to a login/block page; its graph and attribution survive in university teaching material. [Original post](https://research.facebook.com/blog/2016/2/three-and-a-half-degrees-of-separation/), [University of Porto reproduction](https://www.dcc.fc.up.pt/~pribeiro/aulas/ns2122/1_introduction.pdf).

- **High — “Degrees” is used inconsistently; define the unit on screen.** Backstrom and colleagues explicitly use intermediate people: **Quoted definition:** “distance minus one”. Thus **A → B → C** has **two friendship hops and one intermediary**. Compare Facebook’s **4.74 versus 4.57 hops**, or **3.74 versus 3.57 intermediaries**—never 4.74 hops against 3.57 intermediaries. [Backstrom et al., footnote 1](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~mkearns/teaching/NetworkedLife/Backstrom.pdf).

**3. Retellings to avoid**

- “Milgram proved everyone on Earth is six handshakes apart.” Neither the sampling nor the incomplete chains establish that.
- “Most letters arrived.” The verified counts show otherwise.
- “296 letters started in Omaha and Wichita in 1967.” This combines different trials and publication dates.
- “The email experiment directly measured a worldwide average of six.”
- “Facebook proved the entire human population became closer.” It measured its own account network.
- “Kleinfeld proved short paths do not exist.” Her critique challenges what the experiments justify, not every possible small-world effect.

**4. Best hook**

> “Six handshakes to anyone on Earth? The famous experiment never proved that.”

> “The letters that arrived travelled through surprisingly few people. Most letters never arrived.”

**5. Showcase idea**

Animate the verified **1969** flow: **296 recruited → 217 forwarded → 64 arrived**. Then reveal that **5.2 intermediaries** describes the completed chains.

For the single extension, turn the people into Facebook nodes and demonstrate **hops versus intermediate people**. Label this as a different measurement.

Small source line: **Travers & Milgram, 1969 • Backstrom et al., 2012 • Facebook Research, 2016**.

**6. Images**

**None needed.** Original envelope and network animations communicate the evidence more clearly than stock portraits.

**7. Armenian anchor**

**None found** that supports a specifically Armenian separation statistic or historical contribution.

**8. Open questions**

- Karinthy’s wording leaves some ambiguity about whether the selected destination belongs within his five people; Backstrom et al. explicitly acknowledge this.
- No cited result establishes a maximum distance for every human pair.
- The 2016 Facebook figure is verified here through preserved reproductions, not a fresh inspection of its underlying data or accessible original post.

## O5. Two squares of the same grey — checker-shadow illusion

**1. Verdict on the premise**

**True for the designated patches in Adelson’s image.** Their image intensities match even though they usually appear different in lightness.

Accurate wording: **“These two patches contain the same grey in the image. Their surroundings make them look different.”** Avoid implying that every letter, boundary pixel, or compressed copy has identical values.

**2. Key facts**

- **High — Edward H. Adelson’s MIT demonstration dates to 1995.** **Quoted credit:** “©1995, Edward H. Adelson.” [Creator’s MIT page](https://persci.mit.edu/gallery/checkershadow/).

- **High — The equality concerns image values.** Adelson’s own published explanation explicitly distinguishes the pictured surfaces from their pixels. **Quoted evidence:** “the pixel intensities are the same in the image”. [Adelson, 2001, Figure 5 discussion](https://persci.mit.edu/pub_pdfs/adelson_spie_01.pdf).

- **High as the standard explanation — Lightness constancy helps explain the effect.** Vision estimates a surface’s reflectance while accounting for illumination; it does not merely report image intensity. **Quoted terminology:** “lightness constancy mechanisms”; “what is paint and what is shadow”. [Adelson, 2001](https://persci.mit.edu/pub_pdfs/adelson_spie_01.pdf).

- **High — Local contrast contributes.** B is surrounded by darker checks, whereas A is surrounded by lighter checks. **Quoted sentence:** “The first trick is based on local contrast.” [Adelson’s explanation](https://persci.mit.edu/gallery/checkershadow/description/).

- **High — Edge information also contributes.** The explanation distinguishes gradual shadow transitions from sharper surface markings. **Quoted evidence:** “shadows often have soft edges, while paint boundaries (like the checks) often have sharp edges.” This is a cue used by vision, not an infallible rule about all shadows. [Adelson’s explanation](https://persci.mit.edu/gallery/checkershadow/description/).

- **High — The creator provides a revealing comparison.** Connecting the designated patches with matching grey strips makes their equality easier to see. **Quoted evidence:** “two vertical stripes of the same shade of gray”. [MIT proof page](https://persci.mit.edu/gallery/checkershadow/proof/).

- **High — Reuse is permitted, but licences differ between files.** MIT states: **“These checkershadow images may be reproduced and distributed freely.”** Adelson’s named Commons JPEG separately carries **“Attribution 3.0 Unported”**—CC BY 3.0. It is copyrighted, not public domain. [MIT permission](https://persci.mit.edu/gallery/checkershadow/), [Commons licence](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Checkershadow_full_adelson.jpg).

- **High — The 2015 dress study found more than two response categories.** Rosa Lafer-Sousa, Katherine Hermann and Bevil Conway surveyed **1,401 participants** using free descriptions. They reported **57% blue/black, 30% white/gold, 11% blue/brown, and 2% other**. **Quoted entries:** “N = 1401”; “57%”; “30%”; “11%”; “2%”. These describe that sample, not all humanity. [Lafer-Sousa et al., 2015, first page](https://www1.wellesley.edu/sites/default/files/assets/departments/neuroscience/images/lafersousahermannconway_thedress_2015_withsupp-1.pdf).

- **High — Another substantial survey produced different proportions.** Pascal Wallisch’s 2017 paper logged **13,417 participants** in two runs. For recalled *first perception*, white/gold versus black/blue was **59.13% versus 27.19%** in Run 1 and **55.63% versus 30.94%** in Run 2. **Quoted entries:** “13,417”; “59.13%”; “27.19%”; “55.63%”; “30.94%”. Different recruitment and questions prevent treating these as competing estimates of one universal percentage. [Wallisch, 2017, pp. 3–4](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/73de/bc95fb84d04ed3938b095e149ac7579a86cf.pdf).

- **High for association; medium for a complete causal explanation — Lighting assumptions predict dress reports.** Wallisch found perceptions associated with assumptions about shadow and illumination. **Quoted evidence:** “strongly associated with different perceptions of the dress”. This supports an illumination-based account; it does not establish that this is the sole mechanism. [Wallisch, 2017, p. 7](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/73de/bc95fb84d04ed3938b095e149ac7579a86cf.pdf).

**3. Retellings to avoid**

- “Your eyes see the correct colour and your brain deliberately changes it.” The research does not establish that simple processing sequence.
- “It is only the shadow.” Local contrast and other contextual cues matter too.
- “A and B are physically identical real checkerboard tiles.” This is an image demonstration.
- “The dress was evenly split between two camps.” The cited studies disagree in proportions and include additional categories.
- “Dress perception diagnoses personality or left-brain/right-brain dominance.” These studies establish no such diagnostic test.

**4. Best hook**

> “These two squares look different. In the image, they are the same grey.”

> “Your visual system is estimating the surface beneath the shadow.”

**5. Showcase idea**

Move unaltered interior patches from A and B onto a common neutral background, then connect their original locations with a matching grey strip. Change only the context.

For the extension, display the **2015 sample’s four response percentages**, clearly labelled **“Lafer-Sousa et al., 2015; n = 1,401”**.

Small source line: **Edward H. Adelson, 1995/2001 • Lafer-Sousa, Hermann & Conway, 2015**.

**6. Images**

- **High — [File:Checkershadow full adelson.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Checkershadow_full_adelson.jpg)** — Edward H. Adelson; **CC BY 3.0 Unported**. Commons licence wording: “Attribution 3.0 Unported”.
- **High — [File:Checker shadow illusion.svg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Checker_shadow_illusion.svg)** — original by Adelson, vectorized by **Pbroks13**; **CC BY-SA 4.0 International**. Licence wording: “Attribution-Share Alike 4.0 International”. This is a redrawing, not the original raster.

Credit the relevant creators, link the licence, identify changes, and observe ShareAlike where applicable. No dress photograph is needed for the statistical extension.

**7. Armenian anchor**

**None found.**

**8. Open questions**

- **No canonical RGB or hexadecimal value is certified here.** Check the particular production asset before displaying one; avoid lettering, boundaries, scaling artefacts, and JPEG compression.
- The sources support contextual lightness processing, but do not settle every neural mechanism.
- There is no defensible universal white/gold-versus-blue/black percentage from these differently recruited surveys.

## CX47. The surface with only one side — Möbius strip

**1. Verdict on the premise**

**True for the ideal mathematical surface.** A standard Möbius strip has **one side and one boundary component**. A half-twisted paper loop models it; real paper also has thickness.

Accurate wording: **“Give a strip one half-turn before joining its ends, and its surface has only one continuous side.”**

**2. Key facts**

- **High — A half-twist produces the standard one-sided strip.** Join the ends after a **180° half-turn**. Its apparent two edges form one continuous boundary. **Quoted evidence:** “one-sided nonorientable surface”; “half twist”; “the boundary of a Möbius band is a single closed curve.” [MathWorld](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/MoebiusStrip.html), [Plus mathematics explanation](https://plus.maths.org/imaging-maths-inside-klein-bottle).

- **High — Independent mathematical discoveries are credited to 1858.** The names are **August Ferdinand Möbius** and **Johann Benedict Listing**. **Quoted evidence:** “discovered independently in 1858”. [Heinz Klaus Strick’s historical account, hosted by the University of St Andrews, p. 1](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Strick/mobius.pdf).

- **High for priority; medium for an unqualified publication year — Listing published first.** Some histories give **1861**; the separately printed *Der Census räumlicher Complexe* is catalogued **1862**. Möbius’s relevant publication appeared in **1865**. **Quoted dates:** “1861” in the Science News account; “Dieterich, 1862” in the book record; “1865” in Strick. I trust the identifiable edition’s **1862 bibliographic date** for that edition, while retaining the discrepancy. Either account gives Listing publication priority. [Science News](https://www.sciencenews.org/article/m%C3%B6bius-and-his-band), [original-work bibliographic record](https://books.google.de/books?id=WNvNAAAAMAAJ), [Strick](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Strick/mobius.pdf).

- **High — A centre cut produces one longer, two-sided loop.** Its nominal length doubles, its width halves, and it has **four half-twists—two full twists**. **Quoted sentence:** “The result is actually a strip that is twice as long as the original, and with four half-twists instead of one.” [Maths Careers, Institute of Mathematics and its Applications](https://www.mathscareers.org.uk/just-one-side-gets-twist/). The resulting band is described as “two-sided” in [Charu Gupta’s university teaching account](https://cdn.azimpremjiuniversity.edu.in/media/publications/downloads/magazine/At-Right-Angles_Issue-15-Mar23_Online_Low-Res.pdf).

- **High — A cut one-third of the width from the edge produces two linked loops.** For original nominal length **L** and width **W**, the central Möbius band has length **L**, width **W/3**; the other, two-sided band has length **2L**, width **W/3**, and **four half-twists**. Continue the offset cut until it returns to its start. **Quoted table entries:** “Two linked bands”; “One Mobius band”; “Length same as original”; “One band with 4 half twists”; “Length twice the original”. [Gupta, 2023, Table 2, printed p. 10](https://cdn.azimpremjiuniversity.edu.in/media/publications/downloads/magazine/At-Right-Angles_Issue-15-Mar23_Online_Low-Res.pdf).

- **High — Harris’s patent is from 1949, not 1957.** **Owen H. Harris**, US **2,479,929**, concerns an **abrasive belt** and was granted **23 August 1949**. **Quoted evidence:** “This invention relates to an abrasive belt.”; “1949-08-23”. [Original patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2479929A/en).

- **High — The 1957 hot-material conveyor patent is Trinkle’s.** **James O. Trinkle**, assigned to B. F. Goodrich, received US **2,784,834** on **12 March 1957**. Its belt reverses exposed faces through a half-twist. **Quoted evidence:** “1957-03-12”; “one of the ends of the belt is twisted 180 degrees before splicing to the other end.” [Original patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2784834A/en).

- **High — Möbius printer/typewriter ribbons are documented in patents.** Nicholas Kondur Jr.’s US **3,621,968**, granted **23 November 1971**, describes a ribbon cartridge. **Quoted sentence:** “The inking ribbon has a half twist forming a Mobius loop configuration which thereby doubles its effective length.” [Original patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3621968).

- **Medium — The de Forest filmstrip attribution has secondary support; the original grant remains unverified here.** A later patent attributes US **1,442,632** to Lee de Forest for a **“Mobius filmstrip that records sound on both ‘sides’.”** A University of Valencia guide supplies **“1923”** and **“1.442.632”**. I could not inspect the original de Forest patent, so its exact grant details should remain qualified. [Later patent’s historical discussion](https://patents.justia.com/patent/4270907), [Valencia guide, p. 10](https://www.uv.es/puigl/rutesii2ed.pdf).

- **High — Gary Anderson designed the recycling symbol in 1970.** MoMA identifies the work as **“Gary Anderson. Recycling Symbol. 1970”** and describes its design as a **“three-part Möbius strip”**. This supports the design connection; it does not mean every later three-arrow variant depicts the same topology. [MoMA collection record](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/189157), [MoMA design history](https://www.moma.org/explore/inside_out/tag/gary-anderson/).

- **Medium — The Sentinum mosaic is a credible ancient visual comparison, not evidence of ancient topology research.** Julyan Cartwright and Diego González interpret Aion’s zodiacal band as Möbius-shaped. Their figure dates the mosaic to **“ca. 200–250 CE.”** The authors explicitly write that it **“depicts a Möbius strip”**. The artist’s mathematical understanding and intention remain unknown. [Cartwright & González, 2016, Figures 1–2 and discussion](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.07779).

**3. Retellings to avoid**

- “A Möbius strip has no edge.” It has one boundary component.
- “Cutting it down the middle makes two Möbius strips.”
- “The longer loop has one full twist.” For the standard construction, it has **two full twists**.
- “Harris invented the conveyor belt in 1957.” This conflates two patents.
- Turning a patented design into a measured, universal belt-lifetime guarantee.
- “The Romans discovered Möbius topology.” A suggestive depiction does not establish that.
- “Every recycling-arrow graphic is a Möbius strip.”

**4. Best hook**

> “Cut this loop down the middle. You still get one loop.”

> “One half-turn gives this surface just one side—and one continuous edge.”

**5. Showcase idea**

Trace the surface and boundary in different colours. Cut the standard strip along its centreline, then smoothly arrange the result to reveal **one loop with four half-twists**.

Recommended extension: the centre cut. The **one-third cut** can replace it as an alternative showcase; showing both is unnecessary for one short video.

Small source line: **Möbius & Listing, 1858 • Gupta, 2023, Table 2**.

**6. Images**

- **High — [File:Aion mosaic Glyptothek Munich W504.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Aion_mosaic_Glyptothek_Munich_W504.jpg)** — photograph by **Bibi Saint-Pol**; **public domain by the photographer’s dedication**. Commons states: “I, the copyright holder of this work, release this work into the public domain”.

Original strip animations need no external image.

**7. Armenian anchor**

**None found** sufficiently strong to recommend.

**8. Open questions**

- The **1861/1862** bibliographic discrepancy needs resolution before scripting a precise first-publication date. Listing’s priority is secure either way.
- The original **de Forest patent** was not inspected; its commonly cited number and date are not fully certified here.
- The Sentinum artist’s intention is unknown.
- The patents establish disclosed designs, not widespread adoption or independently measured performance benefits.

## CX75. The theremin — an instrument played without touching it

**1. Verdict on the premise**

**True.** A traditional theremin lets the performer control pitch and loudness through hand proximity without touching its playing controls.

Accurate wording: **“Your hands change the electrical behaviour of the instrument. Its circuitry turns those changes into music.”**

**2. Key facts**

- **High — The conventional invention date is 1920, in Soviet Russia.** The inventor was **Lev Sergeyevich Termen**, known internationally as **Leon/Léon Theremin**. **Quoted museum entry:** “1920 - invented the etherophone - an electronic musical instrument - which is played without being touched.” [Science Museum Group](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/people/cp166704/theremin-leon). Use “Soviet Russia” rather than calling the 1920 country the USSR.

- **High — The familiar traditional design has separate pitch and volume antennas.** The upright antenna usually controls pitch; the horizontal loop controls loudness. **Quoted evidence:** “A second antenna”; “controls the volume of the tone”. [Skeldon, Reid, McInally, Dougan & Fulton, 1998, p. 945](https://isidore.co/misc/Physics%20papers%20and%20books/Zotero/storage/3837CDJ6/Skeldon%20et%20al.%20-%201998%20-%20Physics%20of%20the%20Theremin.pdf).

- **High — Classic pitch generation uses heterodyning and capacitance.** Two nearby radio-frequency oscillator frequencies are mixed; the audible component is their difference. Hand proximity changes capacitance and shifts one oscillator. **Quoted evidence:** “a heterodyne mixer which multiplies these two signals”; “electrically influenced by body capacitance”. These are **two oscillators in the pitch explanation**, not necessarily the instrument’s entire oscillator count. [Skeldon et al., pp. 945–946](https://isidore.co/misc/Physics%20papers%20and%20books/Zotero/storage/3837CDJ6/Skeldon%20et%20al.%20-%201998%20-%20Physics%20of%20the%20Theremin.pdf), [Theremin’s patent, claim 5](https://patents.google.com/patent/US1661058A/en).

- **High — The US patent was granted in 1928.** US **1,661,058**, *Method of and apparatus for the generation of sounds*, was filed **5 December 1925** and granted **28 February 1928**. **Quoted patent entries:** “1925-12-05”; “1928-02-28”. The grant date is not the invention date. [Original patent](https://patents.google.com/patent/US1661058A/en).

- **High for the demonstration; medium for the detailed playing anecdote — Lenin encountered it in 1922.** The museum records the Kremlin demonstration in **“1922”**. In a **1989 interview**, Theremin recalled helping Lenin play: **“I took his hands from behind and helped him.”** Then: **“He started to play The Lark.”** Present the playing details as Theremin’s later recollection. [Museum chronology](https://collection.sciencemuseumgroup.org.uk/people/cp166704/theremin-leon), [Olivia Mattis interview, pp. 1–2](https://vasulka.org/archive/Artists9/Theremin-Mattis/interview.pdf).

- **High — Clara Rockmore established its concert potential.** A trained violinist, she developed precise finger movements for controlling pitch and phrasing. **Quoted evidence:** “star performer of the theremin”; “her own finger technique”. Avoid unsupported rankings such as “nobody has ever equalled her”. [Nadia Reisenberg/Clara Rockmore Foundation biography](https://nadiareisenberg-clararockmore.org/clara-rockmore-biography/).

- **High — Both named film scores use theremin.** **Miklós Rózsa’s *Spellbound* (1945)** and **Bernard Herrmann’s *The Day the Earth Stood Still* (1951)** are valid examples. **Quoted evidence:** Rózsa: “Before using it for the first time in Spellbound, I tried to use it twice.” TCM on Herrmann: “To this he added the exotic electronic instrument, the Theremin.” [Rózsa’s 1982 interview](https://miklosrozsa.net/1982Interview/), [TCM film history](https://www.tcm.com/articles/145423/the-day-the-earth-stood-still).

- **High — “Good Vibrations” used an Electro-Theremin, played by Paul Tanner.** It used a mechanical controller rather than touch-free antennas. NAMM describes it as **“operated by a wire in the back and not by waving your hands”**. **Tannerin** more precisely names a later recreation; its maker calls it a **“modern recreation of the Electro-Theremin”**. [Tanner’s NAMM oral-history page](https://www.namm.org/library/oral-history/paul-tanner), [Electro-Theremin maker’s explanation](https://www.electrotheremin.com/etfaq.htm).

- **High for the return year; medium for motive — Theremin returned to the USSR in 1938.** Recent scholarship, drawing on Albert Glinsky’s biography, discusses debt and homesickness as possible motives. **Quoted year:** “1938”. The familiar kidnapping account should not be narrated as settled fact. [Johanna Dahlin, 2025, p. 136](https://www.prometheusjournal.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/PROM_40_3_Dahlin.pdf).

- **High — His imprisonment followed his return.** Dahlin reports arrest in **March 1939** and transfer from Kolyma to a Moscow **sharashka**, a guarded prison workshop, in **December 1939**. **Quoted evidence:** “in March 1939, Theremin was arrested”; “in December 1939”; “prison workshop”. Later movements between facilities are less certain. [Dahlin, 2025, pp. 136–137](https://www.prometheusjournal.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/PROM_40_3_Dahlin.pdf).

- **High — Theremin is credited with the Great Seal listening device, “The Thing”.** It used a passive resonant cavity interrogated by external radio waves. **Quoted evidence:** “Theremin’s solution”; “The microphone hidden inside was passive and only activated when the Soviets wanted it to be.” It did not contain a conventional continuously powered transmitter. [Dahlin, p. 138](https://www.prometheusjournal.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/PROM_40_3_Dahlin.pdf), [NSA National Cryptologic Museum](https://www.nsa.gov/History/National-Cryptologic-Museum/Exhibits-Artifacts/Exhibit-View/Article/2718563/cold-war-great-seal/).

- **High — Prefer the NSA’s gift date and the contemporary discovery record.** NSA dates the presentation to Ambassador Averell Harriman to **“August 4, 1945”**, with the seal remaining **“until 1952”**. Dahlin instead prints **“4 July 1945”**. I favour the specialist museum’s August date. A contemporary FBI report records State Department notification on **“September 11, 1952”**; that is a notification date, not necessarily the recovery day. **3. Retellings to avoid**

- “The antennas read thoughts” or “the hand creates the musical sound directly”.
- “Two high frequencies simply cancel into a low one.” The circuit performs nonlinear mixing; simple linear addition is insufficient.
- “The theremin contains exactly two oscillators.” That describes a simplified pitch circuit.
- “The Beach Boys used a traditional theremin on ‘Good Vibrations’.”
- “Clara Rockmore played the theremin in *Spellbound*.” The sources above do not support that attribution.
- “Theremin was definitely kidnapped from America.” The circumstances remain disputed.
- “The Thing listened continuously for seven years.” Its activation was intermittent; elapsed calendar time does not establish continuous surveillance.
- “The first electronic instrument ever.” Use **“an early electronic instrument”**.

**4. Best hook**

> “This instrument plays without a touch. Move your hands, and you change its electrical circuit.”

> “One hand controls pitch. The other controls loudness.”

**5. Showcase idea**

Animate **two RF oscillators → mixer → filtered difference-frequency sound**. Move a hand toward the pitch antenna and show capacitance changing one oscillator.

**8. Open questions**

- Lenin’s detailed performance story rests here on Theremin’s late recollection, not a contemporaneous performance transcript.
- Theremin’s reasons for returning in 1938 and parts of his prison chronology remain uncertain.
- The exact recovery day of The Thing is not established by the quoted notification record.
